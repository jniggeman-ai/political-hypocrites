BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · Encyclopedia of Arkansas · Cotton.senate.gov

Thomas Bryant Cotton was born May 13, 1977 in Dardanelle, Yell County, Arkansas. He grew up on his family's cattle farm. He graduated from Dardanelle High School in 1995, then attended Harvard University, earning a BA in Government in only three years (1998). He was a columnist for the Harvard Crimson and active in the Harvard Republican Club. He then attended Harvard Law School, earning his JD.

Military service: Cotton served in the U.S. Army from 2004 to 2009. He completed Ranger School and served as an infantry officer. He deployed to Iraq with the 101st Airborne Division in 2006 and to Afghanistan in 2008. After leaving the Army, he worked for McKinsey and Company before entering politics. He is the recipient of the Bronze Star Medal and Combat Infantryman Badge.

Political career: Cotton was first elected to the House in 2012, then ran immediately for the Senate in 2014, defeating Democratic incumbent Mark Pryor 56.5%–39.4%. He was reelected in 2020 running unopposed by any Democratic candidate — an unusual circumstance. In November 2024, Senate Republicans elected him Conference Chair, making him the third-ranking Republican in the Senate. He also chairs the Senate Intelligence Committee. Source: Wikipedia

Iran letter (2015): In March 2015, Cotton organized and signed a letter to the leaders of Iran — co-signed by 47 Republican senators — warning that any nuclear deal signed by President Obama could be overturned by a future president or Congress. Critics called it a violation of the Logan Act, which prohibits private citizens from negotiating with foreign governments. Administration officials and Democrats called it "unprecedented" and "undermining" of diplomatic negotiations. Source: Wikipedia

NY Times op-ed controversy (2020): Cotton wrote a New York Times op-ed calling for military deployment against domestic protesters following the George Floyd protests — titled "Send in the Troops." The publication of the op-ed led to an internal revolt at the Times, the resignation of its opinion editor, and significant public controversy. Source: Wikipedia

NRA lawsuit allegation (2021): A lawsuit against the National Rifle Association alleged the organization used shell corporations to coordinate illegal campaign contributions to Republicans including Cotton. Source: Encyclopedia of Arkansas

War & Foreign Policy RecordSource: Wikipedia · Encyclopedia of Arkansas · Cotton.senate.gov

Iraq (2006): Cotton deployed to Baghdad with the 101st Airborne Division (Air Assault) as an Army infantry officer. He served in the Airborne's Ranger-qualified units and saw combat operations during the Iraq War surge period.

Afghanistan (2008): Cotton deployed to Kunar Province in eastern Afghanistan — one of the most contested areas along the Pakistan border — where Taliban and al-Qaeda forces were highly active.

Arlington National Cemetery (2009): After returning from Afghanistan, Cotton served as an officer in the 3rd U.S. Infantry Regiment (The Old Guard), the unit responsible for ceremonial duties at Arlington National Cemetery. He wrote about this experience in his 2019 book "Sacred Duty."

Iran policy: Cotton has been consistently hawkish on Iran, organizing the 2015 letter to Iranian leaders and repeatedly opposing diplomatic engagement. He co-sponsored legislation to ensure congressional oversight of any Iran nuclear agreement. In 2023 he criticized the Biden administration's diplomatic outreach to China, calling it "embarrassing."

China: Cotton introduced the "Not One More Inch or Acre Act" with Katie Britt in 2023 and 2025, which would ban Chinese nationals and entities from owning American land. He questioned TikTok CEO Shou Zi Chew during 2024 Senate hearings, repeatedly pressing him on CCP membership (Chew affirmed his Singaporean nationality). Source: Wikipedia
