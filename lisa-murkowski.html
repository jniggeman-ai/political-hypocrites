BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · GovTrack · Ballotpedia · OpenSecrets

Lisa Murkowski was born May 22, 1957 in Ketchikan, Alaska — the first person born in Alaska to serve in Congress. Her father, Frank Murkowski, was a U.S. Senator who resigned in 2002 to become Alaska's governor, then appointed his own daughter to fill his vacant Senate seat. She attended Willamette University, then Georgetown University (BA Economics, 1980), and Willamette University College of Law (JD, 1985). She failed the bar exam four times before passing on her fifth attempt. She served in the Alaska House of Representatives from 1999 to 2002 before her Senate appointment.

Write-in victory (2010): Murkowski lost the 2010 Republican primary to Tea Party candidate Joe Miller. Rather than accept the loss, she launched a write-in campaign and defeated both Miller and the Democratic nominee in the general election — one of the few successful Senate write-in campaigns in U.S. history. She was reelected in 2016 and again in 2022.

Political positioning: Murkowski is consistently ranked as one of the most moderate Republicans in the Senate. GovTrack ranks her as the second most liberal Republican senator. The National Journal gave her a composite score of 56% conservative / 45% liberal in 2013. Heritage Action gave her a 34% lifetime score. She is pro-abortion rights, opposes defunding Planned Parenthood, and voted against repealing the ACA without a replacement.

Trump relationship: Murkowski voted to convict Trump in both impeachment trials. She was one of seven Republican senators to vote to convict in the second impeachment. She publicly questioned whether the Republican Party had become "nothing more than the party of Trump" in 2021. When asked in 2024 if she would remain a Republican, she said she was "not attached to a label" and was "navigating my way through some very interesting political times." She has criticized DOGE and said she refuses to compromise her integrity by remaining silent on the Trump administration's second term.

Greenland crisis (Jan 2026): Murkowski traveled to Denmark in January 2026 in a show of support for the country during the Greenland crisis — a notable break from administration policy. Source: Wikipedia

Land ethics issue (2007): Murkowski said she would sell back land she bought from Anchorage businessman Bob Penney after a Senate ethics complaint alleged Penney sold it well below market value. Source: Wikipedia
