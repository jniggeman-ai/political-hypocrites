BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · Sewell.house.gov · GovTrack · OpenSecrets

Terrycina Andrea "Terri" Sewell was born January 1, 1965 in Huntsville, Alabama. She attended Princeton University for her undergraduate degree, Oxford University's St Hilda's College for a second bachelor's degree (later promoted by tradition to an MA), and Harvard Law School for her JD. Before entering politics she was a securities lawyer at Davis Polk & Wardwell and a public finance lawyer at Maynard, Cooper & Gale, where she became the first Black woman to make partner.

Election history: Sewell has represented Alabama's 7th District since 2011, currently in her eighth term. The district includes most of Alabama's Black Belt region, as well as the predominantly African American portions of Birmingham and Tuscaloosa. She is the first African American woman elected to Congress from Alabama, and one of the first women elected to Congress from Alabama in a regular election. She is the only Democrat in Alabama's congressional delegation.

Ways & Means Committee: Sewell sits on the House Ways and Means Committee — one of the most powerful committees in Congress, with jurisdiction over tax policy, trade, Social Security, Medicare, and welfare. In the 119th Congress she serves as Ranking Member of the Subcommittee on Oversight, becoming the first Black woman to hold a leadership role on the committee since it was established in 1789.

Civil rights district: Sewell's first enacted legislation honored the "Four Little Girls" killed in the 1963 16th Street Baptist Church bombing in Birmingham. She has been a consistent advocate for voting rights, introducing the Voting Rights Advancement Act to restore and strengthen protections under the 1965 Voting Rights Act following the Supreme Court's 2013 decision in Shelby County v. Holder.
