Biography

Howard Lutnick grew up in Jericho, New York in a Jewish family. Both of his parents died before he finished college — his mother from lymphoma when he was 16, his father from an accidental chemotherapy overdose during his freshman year at Haverford College. Haverford gave him a full scholarship to finish his degree; a grateful Lutnick later became the college's largest donor. He joined Cantor Fitzgerald straight out of Haverford in 1983 and rose rapidly, becoming president and CEO at just 29 years old after a contentious legal battle with the founder's family over succession.

On September 11, 2001, Lutnick took his five-year-old son to his first day of kindergarten, arriving late to the office and missing the attack on the North Tower — where Cantor Fitzgerald's headquarters were located. The firm lost 658 of its 960 New York-based employees, including Lutnick's brother Gary and his best friend. In the immediate aftermath, Lutnick made a deeply controversial decision: he stopped paychecks to the families of the deceased, arguing the firm needed cash to survive and rebuild. He later reversed course, authorizing a $45 million bonus and establishing the Cantor Fitzgerald Relief Fund, which ultimately distributed $180 million to victims' families. His handling drew both fierce praise and fierce criticism, defining his public image for decades.

Lutnick rebuilt Cantor Fitzgerald from the ground up, expanding into real estate through BGC Partners and Newmark Group. He was a significant Democratic donor for years — giving to Hillary Clinton, Chuck Schumer, and Kamala Harris. By 2020 he had shifted to backing Trump, becoming one of his most visible surrogates on television and co-chairing the 2024 transition team alongside Linda McMahon. He hosted a $57 million fundraiser for Trump at his Hamptons home in August 2024. He was considered for Treasury Secretary before Trump announced him for Commerce instead. He was confirmed 51–45 on February 18, 2025, and immediately handed control of Cantor Fitzgerald to his 27-year-old son Brandon.

As Commerce Secretary, Lutnick has been the most vocal champion of Trump's tariff agenda — publicly calling mainstream economic opposition "nonsense" and stating that a potential recession would be "worth it." He oversees 13 bureaus with 50,000 employees including the Census Bureau, the Patent and Trademark Office, NOAA, and weather forecasting. His advocacy for cryptocurrency while in office has drawn significant scrutiny given Cantor Fitzgerald's deep financial ties to Tether.

Conflicts of Interest
Tether / Cryptocurrency: Cantor Fitzgerald — now run by Lutnick's sons — manages over $130 billion in U.S. Treasury securities that back Tether (USDT), the world's largest stablecoin. Cantor also holds an approximate 5% economic interest in Tether's parent company iFinex, potentially worth hundreds of millions. As Commerce Secretary, Lutnick has championed crypto-friendly legislation including the GENIUS Act (stablecoin regulation). Critics note that laws favorable to Tether directly benefit the Lutnick family's firm. Lutnick's ally Bo Hines left the White House crypto advisor role and immediately became CEO of USAT — Tether's U.S. subsidiary.
800+ business interests: Lutnick's financial disclosure during confirmation revealed positions in more than 800 businesses and private organizations. He pledged to divest within 90 days. Transferring control to his sons rather than selling to unrelated third parties raises ongoing conflict concerns. The status of divestment from most positions remains publicly unclear.
Tariff policy / Cantor Fitzgerald: Cantor Fitzgerald is a major fixed-income trading firm. Tariff and trade policies Lutnick is shaping directly affect bond markets, interest rates, and the Treasury securities Cantor manages. Critics note Lutnick's son's firm benefits from the same macroeconomic environment Lutnick is actively trying to shape.
