BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · GovTrack · Ballotpedia · House History

Rubén Marinelarena Gallego was born November 20, 1979 in Chicago, Illinois, the son of immigrants — his mother from Colombia, his father from Mexico. He was raised by a single mother on a secretary's salary alongside three sisters. He worked jobs at a pizza restaurant, construction sites, and a meatpacking plant to help support his family before earning admission to Harvard University, where he cleaned peers' bathrooms and stacked books to afford school supplies. He graduated with a BA in Government in 2004.

Military service: Gallego served in the U.S. Marine Corps from 2002 to 2006. He deployed to Iraq in 2005 as part of a company that saw some of the highest casualty rates of the war. He wrote about his combat experience in the book "They Called Us 'Lucky': The Life and Afterlife of the Iraq War's Hardest Hit Unit." His military service became central to his political identity and his 2024 Senate campaign.

Political career: Gallego was elected to the Arizona State House in 2010 and the U.S. House of Representatives in 2014. He served five terms in the House representing Phoenix's west side before running for Senate. He was a founding member of the Progressive Caucus and known for sharp, sometimes profane social media posts, though he moved toward the political center in his 2024 Senate campaign.

2024 Senate election: Gallego defeated Republican Kari Lake in November 2024 — outperforming Kamala Harris by 2.4% in Arizona while Harris lost the state by 5.5%. He became the first Latino elected to represent Arizona in the U.S. Senate. He is a founding member of the centrist Majority Democrats group alongside Senators Elissa Slotkin and Michael Bennet.

Missed votes: GovTrack reports Gallego has missed 13.5% of roll call votes across his career — well above the Senate median of 2.8%. Source: GovTrack

War & Foreign Policy RecordSource: Wikipedia · GovTrack · Gallego.senate.gov

Iraq War (2005): Gallego deployed to Iraq as a Marine Corps infantry officer in 2005. His company sustained some of the highest casualty rates of the war. He wrote a book about the experience: "They Called Us 'Lucky': The Life and Afterlife of the Iraq War's Hardest Hit Unit." His combat service is a defining part of his public identity.

Ukraine (2024): Voted for Ukraine military and economic aid while serving in the House. Has been a consistent supporter of Ukraine against Russian aggression.

Israel / Gaza: Gallego voted against the standalone Israel Security Supplemental in the House in 2024 but voted for sanctions on the International Criminal Court after it issued arrest warrants against Israeli leaders in 2025 — a more nuanced position than either full support or full opposition. Source: Wikipedia · GovTrack

El Salvador investigation (April 2025): Gallego joined a group of Democratic House colleagues in traveling to El Salvador to investigate the conditions of Kilmar Abrego Garcia and other migrants deported there by the Trump administration. Source: Wikipedia

Border / immigration: Gallego broke with most Democrats to support the Laken Riley Act and expressed support for deporting "dangerous" migrants to El Salvador and Guantanamo Bay, stating Democrats were "largely out of touch" with the average Latino voter on immigration. Source: Wikipedia
