Background & RecordSource: USDA · Wikipedia · Agriculture Dive · Union of Concerned Scientists

Brooke Rollins grew up on a farm in Glen Rose, Texas — a small town outside Fort Worth. She was deeply involved in 4-H and the Future Farmers of America in high school. She attended Texas A&M University, where she graduated with honors in agricultural development in 1994 and became the first woman ever elected student body president at Texas A&M. She earned her law degree with honors from the University of Texas School of Law.

Texas Public Policy Foundation (2003–2018): Rollins was tapped by Wendy Gramm (wife of Senator Phil Gramm) to run the Texas Public Policy Foundation, a conservative think tank. She grew it from a staff of 3 to a staff of 100 over 15 years. During her tenure, TPPF advocated for ending agriculture subsidies for farmers and opposed ethanol requirements for fuels — positions that came back to haunt her confirmation process. In 2011 Texas Monthly named her one of the 25 most powerful Texans.

Trump White House (2018–2021): She joined Trump's first term as Director of the Office of American Innovation, then became the acting Director of the Domestic Policy Council. She helped develop Trump's first-term domestic policy agenda.

America First Policy Institute (2021–2025): Rollins co-founded AFPI with Larry Kudlow in 2021 as a policy shop to develop the agenda for a potential Trump second term. The organization grew into one of the most influential conservative policy groups in the country. Linda McMahon — also appointed to Trump's Cabinet — was AFPI's chairwoman, meaning two AFPI leaders received Cabinet appointments. Rollins was also briefly considered for White House Chief of Staff, which went to Susie Wiles.

Secretary of Agriculture: Confirmed 72–28. She is only the second woman to serve as USDA Secretary, after Ann Veneman (2001–2005). Progressive groups criticized her appointment, noting she "appears to have no agricultural policy track record" — particularly given TPPF's historical opposition to farm subsidies. The Union of Concerned Scientists called it "another example of President-elect Trump doling out cabinet appointments for loyalty rather than expertise."

As USDA Secretary, she oversees SNAP — the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program — which feeds 42 million Americans including 16 million children and 8 million seniors. She ended the Clinton-era "roadless rule" that had protected 58 million acres of national forest from road construction. She suspended federal funding to Minnesota and Minneapolis in January 2026 over alleged SNAP fraud. She demanded all 50 states provide SNAP recipient data to verify immigration status — 21 states refused and 2 sued.

Financial DisclosuresSource: OGE · Senate Disclosures

AFPI Compensation: Rollins was President and CEO of the America First Policy Institute from 2021 until March 2025. AFPI is a 501(c)(3) and does not disclose its donors or executive compensation publicly. Her income from the organization is detailed in her Senate financial disclosures.

Think Tank Funding Networks: Both TPPF and AFPI are funded by networks of corporate and individual donors with direct interests in USDA policy — including agribusiness, oil and gas (which intersects with farm energy use), and conservative foundations. As USDA Secretary, Rollins now regulates industries that funded the organizations she led for two decades.

Sworn in by Clarence Thomas: Rollins was sworn in by Supreme Court Associate Justice Clarence Thomas — a notable choice that reflects her deep ties to the conservative legal and political establishment.
