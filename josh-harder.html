Background

Early Life & Education

Joshua Keck Harder was born on August 1, 1986 in Turlock, California. His family has been in San Joaquin County for five generations — his great-great-grandfather joined a wagon train west and stopped just short of the gold fields to start a peach farm in Manteca. Harder attended Modesto High School, where — as a teenager — he interned for then-State Senator Jeff Denham, the Republican incumbent he would later defeat for Congress.

He went on to Stanford University, graduating with a B.A. in 2008, then earned a joint MBA and Master of Public Policy from Harvard Business School and the Harvard Kennedy School in 2014.

Pre-Congressional Career

After Harvard, Harder worked as a consultant at The Boston Consulting Group and then as Vice President at Bessemer Venture Partners, one of the oldest venture capital firms in the country. He also taught as a course instructor at Modesto Junior College.

Congressional Career

Harder ran for Congress in 2018 against Republican incumbent Jeff Denham — the same politician he had once interned for. His district (then CA-10) was on the DCCC's targeted list. On election night Denham initially led; Harder pulled ahead as absentee ballots were counted and was projected the winner on November 14, 2018. The seat flip was part of Democrats' House majority gain that cycle.

Following redistricting after the 2020 Census, he ran in the newly drawn CA-9, covering the majority of San Joaquin County including Stockton, Tracy, Lodi, and Manteca. He won re-election in 2022 and 2024, the latter by approximately 9,000 votes against Republican Kevin Lincoln. He serves on the House Appropriations Committee (Interior, Environment, and Related Agencies Subcommittee) and is a member of the New Democrat Coalition and Problem Solvers Caucus.

Harder first ran partly motivated by his brother's healthcare coverage, which was at risk under Republican repeal efforts against the ACA. He has focused his congressional work on agriculture, water, and Central Valley economic issues.

Key Votes

Campaign Finance · 2019
Co-Introduced Ban Corporate PACs Act
In November 2019, Harder co-introduced the Ban Corporate PACs Act with Rep. Max Rose, legislation that would prohibit for-profit corporations from sponsoring, operating, or funding political action committees.
Agriculture
Agriculture Committee Member — Focus on San Joaquin Water & Farming
Harder has served on the Agriculture Committee and focused on water policy, agricultural labor, and farming issues central to his San Joaquin Valley district. The district is one of the most productive agricultural regions in the world.
Attendance · Career
1.5% Missed Vote Rate
From January 2019 through March 2026, Harder missed 54 of 3,642 roll call votes — a career missed vote rate of 1.5%, on par with the 2.1% median among currently serving members.
Post Office Hate Mail · Jan 2021
Received Threatening Mail Intended for Sen. Josh Hawley
After January 6, 2021, Harder received hate mail intended for Sen. Josh Hawley — a Republican senator who objected to certifying Biden's electoral college victory — due to confusion between their similar names. The incident was widely reported as an example of the political environment following the Capitol attack.
