War & Military Action Record Source: Congress.gov · GovTrack.us · State Dept. records
Iran — JCPOA Nuclear Deal OppositionOpposed Deal

Rubio was one of the most vocal Senate opponents of the Iran nuclear agreement, advocating for its collapse and pushing for maximum pressure sanctions. His major donors — Paul Singer, Republican Jewish Coalition, and the Adelson network — were all major funders of campaigns opposing the deal.

⚠️ Iran did not attack the United States. The JCPOA was a multilateral diplomatic agreement to limit Iran's nuclear program in exchange for sanctions relief. Experts widely credited it with delaying Iranian nuclear capability.

Greenland — Did Not Rule Out Military Force (2025)Advocated

As Secretary of State, Rubio stated it was in the U.S. "national interest" to acquire Greenland and explicitly refused to rule out military action to achieve it during congressional testimony and media appearances.

⚠️ Greenland is an autonomous territory of Denmark — a NATO ally since 1949. It has not attacked or threatened the United States. Threatening military force against a NATO ally is a direct violation of the NATO treaty's mutual defense principles.

Yemen / Houthi Military Campaign (2025)Supported Strikes

As Secretary of State, Rubio oversaw the re-designation of the Houthi movement as a Foreign Terrorist Organization and provided diplomatic cover for ongoing U.S. military strikes in Yemen — including strikes that killed civilians.

⚠️ The Houthis began targeting Red Sea shipping in response to the Israeli military campaign in Gaza. Yemen has not attacked the United States directly. No congressional authorization for military force in Yemen was obtained.

ICC Sanctions — International Court Judges (2025)Sanctioned Judges

Rubio announced U.S. sanctions against International Criminal Court judges after the ICC issued arrest warrants for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. This marked the first time the U.S. had sanctioned judges of an international legal body.

⚠️ The ICC is a lawful international institution. Legal scholars and former diplomats from both parties called the sanctions an unprecedented assault on the international rule of law. The U.S. is not an ICC member but 124 other nations are.

Iraq War — AUMF Era (Pre-Senate)Pre-Senate

Rubio was not in the Senate during the 2002 Iraq War AUMF vote. However, he has consistently defended the Iraq War framing and voted for continued military funding throughout his Senate tenure.

⚠️ Iraq did not attack the United States on September 11, 2001. The WMD justification for the invasion was found to be false. Over 4,400 U.S. soldiers and an estimated 185,000+ Iraqi civilians died in the conflict.
