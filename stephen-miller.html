Background & RecordSource: Wikipedia · Britannica · Axios · CNN · NPR

Stephen Miller grew up in a politically liberal Jewish family in Santa Monica, California. By his own account he became conservative in middle school after reading NRA CEO Wayne LaPierre's book. By high school he was appearing on conservative radio talk shows, writing op-eds criticizing his school's "liberal bias," and successfully campaigning to reinstate the Pledge of Allegiance — though only two days a week.

He attended Duke University, graduating in 2007 with a degree in political science. At Duke he was a conservative columnist known for provocative, often inflammatory writing. He then worked as press secretary for Rep. Michele Bachmann and Rep. John Shadegg before joining Senator Jeff Sessions' staff in 2009 — working specifically on immigration policy. He and Sessions, alongside Steve Bannon of Breitbart News, are credited with laying the ideological groundwork for Trump's 2016 victory.

First Trump term (2017–2021): Miller served as senior advisor and director of speechwriting. He co-wrote Trump's "American Carnage" inaugural address with Steve Bannon. He was the primary architect of the Muslim travel ban — writing it in secret, without interagency review, producing the chaotic airport scenes of January 2017. He was the driving force behind family separation — the policy of taking children from parents who crossed the border illegally — which resulted in thousands of children being separated from their families, some of whom were never reunited. He supported firing FBI Director James Comey and drafted the letter doing so.

America First Legal (2021–2025): After Trump left office, Miller founded America First Legal, a conservative legal organization that contributed to Project 2025 — the Heritage Foundation's blueprint for a second Trump term. Trump publicly distanced himself from Project 2025 during the campaign while key figures like Miller were writing it.

Second term (2025–present): Miller serves as Deputy Chief of Staff for Policy and Homeland Security Advisor — neither requires Senate confirmation. He is considered the central architect of the administration's immigration crackdown. He has demanded ICE reach 3,000 arrests per day — triple the first-term rate. He has pushed for mass deportations, military detention centers, and stripping documents from legal immigrants. When a federal judge ordered deportation flights to El Salvador to stop, Miller directed them to continue in defiance of the court order. He has pressured DOJ and FBI leadership on personnel, wielding extraordinary influence for an unelected advisor. He has publicly stated "America is for Americans and Americans only." He has also said the president has "plenary authority" — meaning absolute power.
