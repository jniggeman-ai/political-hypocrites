Background & RecordSource: Wikipedia · OpenSecrets · PBS · Georgia Recorder

Kelly Loeffler grew up in Bloomington, Illinois, earned a degree from the University of Illinois, and an MBA from DePaul University. She built her career in financial services, eventually becoming CEO of Bakkt — a cryptocurrency and digital payments company — which is a subsidiary of the Intercontinental Exchange (ICE). Her husband, Jeffrey Sprecher, is the founder and CEO of ICE and chairman of the New York Stock Exchange.

Senate appointment (2020–2021): Loeffler had essentially zero political experience when Georgia Governor Brian Kemp appointed her to fill Senator Johnny Isakson's seat in December 2019. She served approximately one year before losing the January 2021 runoff to Raphael Warnock. During her brief Senate tenure she was the wealthiest member of the Senate. She described herself as "more conservative than Attila the Hun" to fend off primary challenges from her right.

COVID stock trades: Shortly after receiving a classified Senate briefing on COVID-19 in January 2020, Loeffler sold millions of dollars in stock before markets crashed. She was ultimately cleared of wrongdoing by the Senate Ethics Committee and DOJ, but the optics drew intense national attention and damaged her reelection bid.

January 6th: Loeffler announced she would object to the certification of Electoral College results. She reversed course after the Capitol attack, voting to certify and stating "I cannot now in good conscience object." She called for Georgia Secretary of State Brad Raffensperger's resignation after he certified Biden's victory — contributing to Republican voter suppression narratives ahead of the Georgia runoffs, which Democrats then won.

Post-Senate: She founded Greater Georgia, a conservative voter registration organization, and became one of Trump's biggest donors and bundlers for his 2024 campaign. She co-chaired Trump's second inaugural committee. She was confirmed as SBA Administrator on a 52–46 party-line vote. She donated her government salary to charity. In June 2025 — after her confirmation — she and her husband each donated $2.5 million to MAGA Inc., bringing their combined post-appointment Trump giving to $5 million.

Financial Disclosures & Stock ControversySource: OGE · Senate Ethics · OpenSecrets

COVID stock trades: Shortly after a classified January 2020 Senate briefing on COVID-19, Loeffler sold between $1.28 million and $3.1 million in stock before markets crashed in February and March. She and her husband purchased shares in companies that benefited from remote work and PPE demand. She was cleared by the Senate Ethics Committee and DOJ of insider trading, but the trades drew intense national scrutiny.

Net worth: Loeffler was the wealthiest member of the U.S. Senate during her tenure. Her wealth is tied largely to her husband Jeffrey Sprecher's stake in Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), which owns the New York Stock Exchange. Their Atlanta estate was purchased for $10.5 million — the most expensive residential real estate transaction ever recorded in Atlanta at the time.

Bakkt: As CEO of Bakkt — a crypto and digital payments subsidiary of ICE — Loeffler had significant exposure to the cryptocurrency industry. As SBA Administrator she now operates in an administration that has made crypto deregulation a priority, creating potential conflicts given her background.
