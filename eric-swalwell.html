Background

Early Life & Education

Eric Michael Swalwell was born on November 16, 1980 in Sac City, Iowa — the oldest of four sons. His father was a police chief in Iowa before the family relocated to Dublin, California, in the East Bay. Notably, both of his parents are Republicans. He suffered from Bell's palsy as a child and wore an eye patch during treatment. A soccer scholarship allowed him to become the first in his family to attend college; he played for the Campbell University Fighting Camels in North Carolina before transferring to the University of Maryland, where he interned on Capitol Hill for Rep. Ellen Tauscher. He earned his law degree from the University of Maryland School of Law.

Pre-Congressional Career

After law school, Swalwell became a prosecutor in the Alameda County District Attorney's Office, where he led the Hate Crimes Unit. He was elected to the Dublin City Council in 2010, serving as one of the youngest city council members in Dublin's history.

Congressional Career

In 2012, Swalwell ran against 20-term incumbent Rep. Pete Stark, a Democrat, in California's top-two primary. He finished second behind Stark and advanced to the general, then defeated him 52%–48% — one of the rare instances of a House member defeating an incumbent of the same party in a general election. He has represented the East Bay district (Dublin, Fremont, Castro Valley, and surrounding communities) since January 2013.

Swalwell served eight years on the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence, becoming lead Democrat on the CIA subcommittee. He led the House investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 election and was appointed as an impeachment manager for Trump's second Senate trial following January 6, 2021. He also filed a civil lawsuit in March 2021 against Trump, Donald Trump Jr., Rep. Mo Brooks, and Rudy Giuliani for their alleged role in inciting the January 6 attack.

He ran for the 2020 Democratic presidential nomination from April to July 2019 — a 93-day campaign — before dropping out and endorsing Joe Biden. He co-chairs the House Democratic Steering Committee and founded Future Forum, a group of young Democratic members focused on millennial issues.

In November 2025, Swalwell announced he would not seek re-election to the House and is instead running for Governor of California in 2026. As of early 2026 he leads Democratic primary polling in that race.

Extended Absence · Fall 2025

Between September 19, 2025 and January 21, 2026, Swalwell missed 97 of 119 roll call votes — an 82% absence rate during that period. GovTrack does not track the reasons for absences. His career missed-vote rate is 8.7%, well above the 2.1% median among currently serving members.

Notable Controversies

National Security · December 2020
Targeted by Suspected Chinese Intelligence Operative Fang Fang
In December 2020, Axios reported that a Chinese national known as Fang Fang (or Christine Fang), believed by U.S. intelligence to be an operative of China's Ministry of State Security, had cultivated a relationship with Swalwell beginning around 2012 — when he was a Dublin city council member — and continuing through his early congressional career. According to Axios, Fang participated in fundraising for Swalwell's 2014 re-election campaign, helped place at least one intern in his congressional office, and interacted with him at multiple events over several years. Around 2015, the FBI gave Swalwell a defensive briefing alerting him to concerns about Fang; he immediately cut off all contact. Fang left the country shortly thereafter. The Axios report stated Swalwell was not accused of any wrongdoing, officials did not believe classified information was obtained from him, and the San Francisco Chronicle quoted an unnamed FBI official saying Swalwell was "completely cooperative and under no suspicion of wrongdoing." The House Ethics Committee opened an inquiry and closed it in May 2023 without finding any wrongdoing. Speaker Kevin McCarthy cited the Fang episode when he removed Swalwell from the Intelligence Committee in January 2023; Swalwell called it "purely vengeance."
Rules Violation · June 2013
Violated House Rules by Recording Vote on Mobile Phone
During a June 18, 2013 House vote, Swalwell used his mobile phone to record a six-second video of himself pressing the "nay" button on the electronic voting machine and uploaded it to the social media platform Vine. House rules bar "the use of mobile electronic devices that impair decorum" and prohibit audio or video recording on the floor. The episode was widely noted as an early example of the collision between social media norms and House rules.

Key Votes

Impeachment · January 2021
House Impeachment Manager — Trump's Second Impeachment
Swalwell was appointed as one of the nine House impeachment managers who prosecuted Trump's second Senate trial following the January 6 Capitol attack. The charge was incitement of insurrection. The Senate voted 57–43 to convict — a majority, but short of the two-thirds needed — resulting in acquittal.
Lawsuit · March 2021
Filed Civil Suit Against Trump, Trump Jr., Brooks, and Giuliani for Jan. 6
On March 5, 2021, Swalwell filed a civil lawsuit seeking damages against President Trump, Donald Trump Jr., Rep. Mo Brooks (R-AL), and Rudy Giuliani, alleging their actions incited the January 6 attack on the Capitol.

Intelligence · 8 Years
House Intelligence Committee — CIA Subcommittee Lead
Swalwell served on the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence for eight years, including as lead Democrat on the subcommittee with CIA oversight. He was involved in the committee's investigation of Russian interference in the 2016 election.
Russia Investigation
Led House Investigation into 2016 Russian Election Interference
Swalwell was among the most prominent House Democrats advocating publicly for a robust investigation of Russian interference in the 2016 election. He made numerous media appearances asserting the seriousness of Russia's actions, and was a vocal critic of the Trump administration's posture toward Russia.
