Background

Early Life & Education

Rohit "Ro" Khanna was born in 1976 in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, to Indian immigrant parents. His father is a chemical engineer; his mother was a substitute school teacher. His grandfather was active in Gandhi's independence movement and spent time in prison for promoting human rights in India — an influence Khanna frequently cites. He graduated Phi Beta Kappa with a B.A. in economics from the University of Chicago in 1998, where he worked on the campaign of a then-little-known state senate candidate named Barack Obama. He earned his J.D. from Yale Law School in 2001.

Pre-Congressional Career

Khanna taught economics at Stanford University and adjunct law at Santa Clara University. President Obama appointed him Deputy Assistant Secretary of Commerce in 2009, where he organized clean technology trade missions and expanded the Green Embassy program. He ran unsuccessfully for CA-17 in 2014, losing to incumbent Mike Honda by about 4,700 votes. He won the seat in 2016, defeating Honda in the general election.

Congressional Career

Khanna has served since January 2017 and is now in his fifth term. He is Vice Chair of the Congressional Progressive Caucus and serves on the House Armed Services Committee as Ranking Member of the Cybersecurity, Innovative Technologies and Information Systems (CITI) Subcommittee, and on the Select Committee on Strategic Competition with China. He co-founded the No PAC Caucus in 2017 with Beto O'Rourke and Jared Polis and takes no corporate PAC contributions. He co-chaired Bernie Sanders's 2020 presidential campaign. He authored the Endless Frontier Act, which became the basis for the CHIPS and Science Act, and authored the Epstein Files Transparency Act — which was enacted into law.

PAC Policy
No Corporate PAC Money — Co-Founded No PAC Caucus
Khanna co-founded the No PAC Caucus in 2017 and accepts no corporate PAC contributions. His fundraising comes from individual donors, heavily concentrated in the technology and securities industries of his Silicon Valley district.

Key Votes

Legislation · 2022
Authored Endless Frontier Act — Foundation of CHIPS and Science Act
Khanna authored the Endless Frontier Act, which formed the legislative basis for the bipartisan CHIPS and Science Act signed by President Biden in 2022 — investing $52 billion in domestic semiconductor manufacturing and $200 billion in scientific research.
Transparency · Enacted
Authored Epstein Files Transparency Act — Enacted into Law
Khanna authored H.R. 4405, the Epstein Files Transparency Act, which was enacted into law. The legislation requires the release of federal government records related to the Jeffrey Epstein case.
Attendance
0.8% Career Missed Vote Rate
From January 2017 through March 2026, Khanna missed 38 of 4,861 roll call votes — a rate of 0.8%, among the lowest of any currently serving member.
2016 Campaign
First Campaign Heavily Funded by Silicon Valley Tech Elite
His 2014 run was backed by major Silicon Valley figures including Marissa Mayer, Sheryl Sandberg, Eric Schmidt, Sean Parker, and Marc Andreessen — while his 2016 and subsequent campaigns have relied on individual donors under contribution caps, with no PAC money.

War & Foreign Policy

China Policy
Ranking Member — CITI Subcommittee · Select Committee on China
Khanna is Ranking Member of the Armed Services Cybersecurity subcommittee and serves on the Select Committee on Strategic Competition with China. He supports rebalancing U.S.-China trade relations and shoring up domestic manufacturing — including through the CHIPS Act — while also advocating for diplomatic engagement over military confrontation.
Military Restraint
Progressive Foreign Policy — Against Open-Ended Military Commitments
Khanna advocates for what he describes as military restraint and diplomatic engagement, arguing the U.S. should invest in domestic priorities rather than prolonged overseas wars. He has supported War Powers resolutions to limit unauthorized military action.
Ukraine · 2023
Signed Letter Urging Biden to Provide F-16s to Ukraine
In February 2023, Khanna was among members who signed a letter advocating for President Biden to provide F-16 fighter jets to Ukraine in support of its defense against Russia's invasion.
