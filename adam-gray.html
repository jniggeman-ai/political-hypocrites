Background

Early Life & Education

Adam Channing Gray was born on September 23, 1977 in Merced, California — where he still lives. He grew up working in his family's dairy supply and feed store. He attended Golden Valley High School in Merced as part of its first graduating class. He earned a B.A. in Political Science from UC Santa Barbara. He is currently an educator at the University of California, Merced.

He began his political career as a legislative staffer, working in the district office of state Assemblymember Dennis Cardoza and later as a staffer for Assemblymembers Herb Wesson, Fabian Núñez, and Jerome Horton, and state Senator Ron Calderon. He was subpoenaed to testify as a witness in Calderon's federal corruption trial in 2015 — he was a witness, not a defendant, and was not accused of wrongdoing.

California State Assembly (2012–2022)

Gray was elected to the California State Assembly in 2012, representing the 21st District covering Merced and Stanislaus Counties. He served for ten years. In Sacramento, he founded the California Problem Solvers Caucus — a bipartisan effort to pass legislation across party lines. He was involved in securing $3 billion for water storage in the Central Valley and helped establish a medical school program at UC Merced. He broke with his party on several water policy issues, pushing back against Democratic proposals to restrict water deliveries to San Joaquin Valley irrigation districts — a stance that resulted in him being removed from his chairmanship of the Assembly Government Organization Committee by the Democratic Assembly leader.

Congressional Career — Two Races, Two Different Outcomes

Gray ran for California's newly redrawn 13th Congressional District in 2022 against Republican pistachio farmer John Duarte. The race was one of the last called in the nation; Gray conceded on December 2, 2022 after losing by 564 votes — the second-closest House race nationally that cycle.

In 2024, Gray ran again against Duarte in a direct rematch. Weeks of ballot counting followed election night; Gray took the lead on November 26 and the race was called for him on December 4, 2024 by a margin of 187 votes — the closest House race in the entire country that election cycle. The district voted for Donald Trump in the 2024 presidential election while simultaneously electing Gray, one of only 13 House districts nationally with that split result.

Gray took office on January 3, 2025. He was named Whip of the Blue Dog Coalition and serves on the House Agriculture and Natural Resources Committees.

Notable Context

Real Estate · August 2024
Purchased Property Near State-Funded Redevelopment Site
The Los Angeles Times reported in August 2024 that Gray had purchased real estate shortly before a $50 million state-funded redevelopment of 70 acres at the former Castle Air Force Base in Merced County was announced. The report raised questions about whether the purchase was timed with knowledge of the redevelopment plan. Gray's campaign did not respond to requests for comment on the report at the time.
Personal Connection
Previously Married to Daughter of Gary Condit
Gray was previously married to Cadee Condit, daughter of former Congressman Gary Condit. Gray had interned for Gary Condit while in school. Condit, a Central Valley Democrat, was a focus of intense media scrutiny in 2001 in connection with the disappearance and murder of intern Chandra Levy; he was never charged with any crime, and another man was convicted of the killing. Gray and Cadee Condit are no longer married.

Key Votes

Immigration · January 2025
Voted for Laken Riley Act — One of 46 Democrats
Gray was one of 46 House Democrats who joined all Republicans to vote for the Laken Riley Act of 2025, which requires federal immigration authorities to detain undocumented immigrants charged with or convicted of certain crimes. The bill was criticized by progressive Democrats as overreach but backed by centrists in competitive districts.
Water · 2025
Introduced Valley Water Protection Act
Gray introduced the Valley Water Protection Act, which would limit implementation of the Endangered Species Act in cases where it could pose a national security risk or cause significant regional economic harm. The bill reflects the Central Valley's long-running tension between federal environmental protections and water delivery to farmers.
Water · Bipartisan
Co-Sponsored Two Bipartisan Groundwater Storage Bills
On the House Natural Resources Committee, Gray co-sponsored two bipartisan bills to expand federal support for groundwater storage and recharge efforts in California — a priority for the heavily agricultural San Joaquin Valley.
