Biography

Kristi Lynn Arnold was born on November 30, 1971, in Watertown, South Dakota. She grew up on a family farm and ranch in Castlewood, South Dakota, and attended South Dakota State University but did not graduate. She married Bryon Noem in 1992. She began her political career in the South Dakota House of Representatives in 2007, then was elected to the U.S. House of Representatives in 2010, representing South Dakota's at-large congressional district until 2019.

She was elected the first female Governor of South Dakota in 2018 with Trump's endorsement and re-elected in 2022. She gained national attention during the COVID-19 pandemic for opposing statewide mask mandates and keeping South Dakota's economy fully open — a stance that drew praise from conservatives and criticism from public health officials. She became a frequent Fox News presence and national conservative media figure, widely seen as a potential vice presidential candidate.

Her vice presidential prospects collapsed in spring 2024 when an advance copy of her memoir "No Going Back" revealed she had shot and killed her 14-month-old family dog and castrated a goat. Even Trump was reportedly "disgusted" by the disclosure. Despite this, Trump nominated her as DHS Secretary in November 2024. She was confirmed 59–34 on January 25, 2025 — with seven Democrats crossing over to vote for her.

Her tenure at DHS was marked by an aggressive immigration enforcement posture, high-profile social media posts from immigration raids, lavish government spending, an alleged relationship with Trump advisor Corey Lewandowski, and the fatal shooting of two U.S. citizens by federal agents in Minneapolis. Trump fired her on March 5, 2026 — the first Cabinet secretary fired in his second term — and nominated Senator Markwayne Mullin to replace her.

Why She Was Fired — March 5, 2026
Minneapolis: Two U.S. citizens killed by federal agents. In January 2026, CBP and ICE agents shot and killed Renée Good and Alex Pretti — both U.S. citizens — during immigration enforcement operations in Minneapolis. Noem immediately and publicly declared them "domestic terrorists" before any investigation had taken place. Subsequent investigation indicated the characterization was unfounded. Even Republican senators demanded she apologize. She repeatedly refused. Senator Thom Tillis (R-NC) publicly called for her resignation: "The fact that you can't admit to a mistake which looks like, under investigation, is gonna prove that Ms. Good and Mr. Pretti probably should not have been shot in the face and in the back!"
$220 million advertising contracts. DHS under Noem spent $220 million on advertising and public relations contracts — a figure that drew bipartisan outrage. Much of the spending appeared to be on immigration-themed ads and content promoting Noem's own image. Congressional investigators questioned whether the spending violated rules against using federal funds for self-promotion.
Corey Lewandowski relationship allegations. Reports emerged of an alleged personal relationship between Noem and Corey Lewandowski, a longtime Trump advisor who was serving as a DHS aide. Lewandowski had previously been accused of sexual misconduct by a Republican donor in 2021. He reportedly fired a Coast Guard pilot mid-mission because Noem's blanket had been left on a different aircraft — an extraordinary exercise of authority that DHS denied occurred.
Obstructed 10 internal investigations. The DHS Inspector General found at least 10 instances in which Noem had stonewalled or obstructed internal DHS investigations. Senator Tillis cited this directly in his public call for her resignation. She also allegedly diverted $80,000 from a nonprofit to her personal company while governor and failed to disclose this on federal ethics forms.
Living rent-free in Coast Guard residence. Noem was living in the Coast Guard Commandant's residence at Joint Base Anacostia-Bolling. DHS claimed she was paying fair market value but provided no documentation.
Key Controversies as Governor
Controversy
Used her position to secure a real estate appraiser license for her daughter Kassidy Peters.
In 2021, the South Dakota Department of Labor moved to deny Peters a real estate appraiser trainee license because she lacked qualifying experience. Noem arranged a meeting between department officials and Peters and her husband at the Governor's residence. The license was subsequently approved. The state's appraiser certification manager — who had flagged the rejection — was forced out of her position afterward. A federal investigation was opened.
Ethics Violation
Funneled $80,000 from a nonprofit into her personal company.
In 2023, while serving as governor, Noem funneled $80,000 in fees from the American Resolve Policy Fund — a nonprofit — into her personal company. She failed to disclose this payment in her federal ethics filings upon joining DHS, which ethics experts say violates federal disclosure rules.
False
Claimed in her book to have met with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un.
Her 2024 memoir "No Going Back" included a claim that she had met with Kim Jong-un. This was false. She had not met Kim Jong-un. The claim was removed from subsequent printings of the book.
Ongoing
"ICE Barbie" / social media photo ops.
Noem was criticized by ICE and Border Patrol agents themselves for her frequent social media posts from immigration operations — wearing field clothing and a $50,000 gold Rolex watch during a visit to El Salvador's CECOT prison. Agents complained she treated enforcement operations as photo opportunities. Critics dubbed her "ICE Barbie" and "Homeland Barbie."
"No Going Back" — The Memoir (2024)
The dog. Noem's 2024 memoir revealed that she shot and killed her family's 14-month-old dog, Cricket, in a gravel pit. She described Cricket as "untrainable" and "less than worthless" after the dog attacked chickens during a hunting trip. She wrote: "I hated that dog." She also described castrating a "nasty and mean" family goat. She framed these as examples of making "tough decisions" — a leadership lesson. The story prompted outrage across the political spectrum. Trump was reportedly "disgusted" and asked, "What is wrong with her?" Despite this, he nominated her for DHS Secretary.
The Kim Jong-un claim. The book included a claim that Noem had met with North Korean dictator Kim Jong-un. This was false. The story was removed from later printings without explanation. No retraction or explanation was offered.
Republican senator's verdict. During her Senate Judiciary Committee hearing in March 2026, Republican Senator Thom Tillis directly connected the dog incident to her DHS leadership: "You are a farmer. You should know better... I train dogs, alright?... Those are bad decisions made in the heat of the moment. Not unlike what happened up in Minneapolis." He called for her resignation. One week after Trump fired her, Noem posted a photo of a dog on Instagram — captioned "Sweet Foster. DHS Ambassador" with a heart emoji.
