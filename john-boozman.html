BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · GovTrack · Ballotpedia · Boozman.senate.gov

John Nichols Boozman was born December 10, 1950 in Shreveport, Louisiana, where his father was stationed with the U.S. Air Force. His family returned to Fort Smith, Arkansas, where he was raised. He attended the University of Arkansas, where he played football for the Razorbacks, then graduated from the Southern College of Optometry. He co-founded a private optometry clinic in 1977 and worked as a volunteer optometrist for low-income families. Before politics he served two terms on the Rogers Public School Board. He is the brother of the late state senator Fay Boozman.

Congressional career: Boozman was first elected to Congress in a 2001 special election after his predecessor Asa Hutchinson resigned to become head of the DEA. He became the first Republican to represent his northwestern Arkansas district since Reconstruction. He served in the House until 2010, when he defeated Democratic incumbent Senator Blanche Lincoln by 21 points — becoming only the second Republican ever popularly elected to the U.S. Senate from Arkansas. He was reelected in 2016 (59.8%) and 2022 (65.8%). He is the dean of Arkansas's congressional delegation. Source: Wikipedia

Iraq War vote (2002): In October 2002, Boozman voted in favor of the Iraq Resolution authorizing the use of force. In 2011, after 4,500 Americans and hundreds of thousands of civilians had died in the war, he stated: "Right now, Iraq has a stable police force, a stable military." Source: Wikipedia

January 6 / 2020 election: Boozman and Tom Cotton initially indicated they might object to the 2020 electoral vote certification. After the January 6 Capitol attack, they changed course and voted to support certification. Boozman said: "The events that transpired in Washington were not only shocking and unlawful, but represent a dark moment in our country's history." Source: Wikipedia
