Background

Early Life

Lateefah Aaliyah Simon was born on January 29, 1977 in San Francisco, California. She grew up in public housing. She was born legally blind in both eyes — a condition she has had her entire life — and has never been able to drive. Her reliance on public transit shaped her later advocacy and her decision to run for the BART Board of Directors.

At 16, she began working as an outreach coordinator for the Young Women's Freedom Center (YWFC) in San Francisco, an organization serving marginalized young women and trans youth. At 18, she became pregnant and raised her eldest daughter, Aminah, as a single mother while continuing her work. By age 19, she had become Executive Director of the YWFC. She later earned a B.A. in Public Policy and a Master of Public Administration from the University of San Francisco.

MacArthur Fellowship & Advocacy Career

In 2003, at age 26, Simon received a MacArthur Foundation Fellowship — commonly known as the "Genius Grant" — for her leadership of the Young Women's Freedom Center. She became the youngest woman ever to receive the award. She was later tapped by then-San Francisco District Attorney Kamala Harris to lead the creation of "Back on Track," a first-of-its-kind anti-recidivism program for young adults charged with low-level offenses.

Simon subsequently served as Executive Director of the Lawyers' Committee for Civil Rights of the San Francisco Bay Area, Program Director of the Rosenberg Foundation, President of the Akonadi Foundation (an Oakland-based racial justice philanthropic organization), and President of MeadowFund. She was appointed by Governor Jerry Brown to the Board of Trustees of the California State University system. Her husband, journalist Kevin Weston, died of cancer in 2014, leaving her with nearly $1 million in medical debt; her East Bay community rallied to support her family through the crisis.

BART Board

In 2016, galvanized by the police killing of Oscar Grant, Simon ran for and was elected to the Bay Area Rapid Transit Board of Directors' District 7 — partly motivated by her own daily reliance on BART as someone who is legally blind and cannot drive. She served as BART Board President from 2020 to 2024, steering the agency through the COVID-19 crisis. She launched BART's Ambassador Program, a nationally recognized public safety model using unarmed ambassadors and crisis intervention specialists. In 2022, BART briefly removed her from the board after determining her home was outside district boundaries due to a move prompted by threats against her family — a decision BART reversed weeks later after investigation.

Congressional Career

Simon was elected to Congress in 2024, winning Barbara Lee's CA-12 seat with 63% of the vote. She was sworn in by Vice President Kamala Harris ceremonially, and formally sworn in by Speaker Mike Johnson with her hand on the Quran — a notable moment in the deeply Democratic East Bay district. She is the first member of Congress known to be born legally blind in both eyes and the first Muslim member of Congress from California.

She serves as Deputy Whip for Policy and Vice Chair of the Congressional Progressive Caucus, and is a member of the Congressional Black Caucus and Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus. She was assigned to the House Oversight Committee.

Top Donors & Funding

Key Votes

Bipartisan · Early 2025
Co-Sponsored Bill with Republican to Bar PPP Fraudsters from SBA Aid
Within weeks of taking office, Simon co-sponsored legislation with Rep. Roger Williams (R-TX), chair of the House Small Business Committee, that would bar individuals convicted of defrauding the government — including through fraudulent Paycheck Protection Program loans — from receiving any future Small Business Administration assistance.
Public Safety · BART
Launched Nationally Recognized BART Ambassador Program
As BART Board President, Simon launched the Ambassador Program — an unarmed public safety model integrating transit ambassadors and crisis intervention specialists to improve safety and accessibility on BART. The program has been cited nationally as a model for alternative public safety approaches.
Attendance · Partial Career
2.5% Missed Vote Rate (First Year)
From January 2025 through February 2026, Simon missed 11 of 440 roll call votes — a rate of 2.5%, on par with the 2.1% median among currently serving members.

War & Foreign Policy

General Posture
Progressive Caucus — Critical of Military Interventionism
As Vice Chair of the Congressional Progressive Caucus, Simon is expected to align with the caucus's generally skeptical stance on overseas military interventions and unauthorized uses of force. Her district — CA-12 in Alameda County — is one of the most progressive in the country.
Israel / Palestine
Succeeded Barbara Lee — Strong Pro-Ceasefire District
Simon succeeded Barbara Lee, who was among the earliest congressional voices calling for a permanent ceasefire in Gaza. Simon's district has a highly engaged progressive base that has been vocal on the issue. Her foreign policy positions on the conflict are expected to be monitored closely as she develops her record.
