Background & RecordSource: DOE · Wikipedia · OpenSecrets · Energy and Policy Institute

Chris Wright grew up in Colorado and earned a mechanical engineering degree from MIT, then did graduate work in electrical engineering at MIT and UC Berkeley. He is the only member of the Trump Cabinet with an MIT degree.

Career in fracking: In 1992, Wright founded Pinnacle Technologies, which created the hydraulic fracture mapping industry and helped launch commercial shale gas production in the late 1990s. He was also chairman of Stroud Energy, an early shale gas producer, until 2006. In 2011 he founded Liberty Energy (originally Liberty Oilfield Services), which grew under his leadership to become the second-largest hydraulic fracturing company in North America — fracking approximately 20% of all U.S. onshore oil and gas wells. The company was valued at $2.8 billion as of 2023, and Wright earned $5.6 million as CEO that year.

Climate positions: Wright is among the most outspoken climate skeptics ever appointed to lead an energy department. In a January 2023 LinkedIn video, he stated "There is no climate crisis and we're not in the midst of an energy transition either." He has called mainstream climate advocacy a movement "collapsing under its own weight." He prefers the term "climate realist" over "climate denier." After becoming Energy Secretary, he said "too little" CO₂ in the atmosphere is a "bigger risk" than rising levels — a claim with no basis in peer-reviewed science — and falsely claimed climate change does not cause more frequent extreme weather events.

The fracking fluid stunt: In 2019, Wright and Liberty Energy employees drank fracking fluid on video to demonstrate it was "safe." Fracking fluid contains bleach, soap, and hazardous chemicals. The stunt was widely mocked by scientists and safety experts.

Koch network ties: Wright has deep ties to the Charles Koch political donor network. In 2014 he co-wrote an op-ed with his wife saying "we proudly stand with the Kochs." He attended Koch donor summits and described Trump's first-term potential as "three parts good, one part bad." The Koch network and Heritage Foundation — which wrote Project 2025 — endorsed his nomination as Energy Secretary.

Oklo conflict: Before his appointment, Wright served on the board of Oklo, a nuclear microreactor company. After he became Energy Secretary, Oklo was selected for multiple pilot projects under a new DOE reactor program he oversees. Oklo's lobbying spending surged 1,200% in the year before his appointment, from $30,000 to $390,000.

Never Trump to Trump: Wright did not support Trump in 2016. He and his wife both said they would not give money to Trump. His wife voted for the Libertarian candidate. By 2024 he had donated $747,000 to the Trump 47 Committee and the RNC.

Financial Disclosures & ConflictsSource: OGE · OpenSecrets · Senate Disclosures

Liberty Energy stake: Wright founded Liberty Energy and held a significant ownership stake when appointed. As Energy Secretary he now oversees federal energy policy that directly affects the fracking industry his company dominates. Liberty Energy fracks approximately 20% of all U.S. onshore wells — making it one of the most directly government-regulated companies whose founder now runs the regulatory apparatus.

Oklo conflict: Wright served on the board of Oklo, a nuclear microreactor startup, before his appointment. After becoming Energy Secretary, Oklo was selected for DOE pilot reactor projects and its lobbying spend surged from $30,000 to $734,000 in the 18 months surrounding his appointment. Wright was required to recuse himself from Oklo-related decisions, but the company has nonetheless benefited substantially from DOE priorities he set.

Federal Reserve Bank of New York: Wright served on the board of the NY Fed from 2020 to 2024 — his only prior government-adjacent role before becoming Energy Secretary.
