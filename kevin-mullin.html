Background

Early Life & Education

Kevin Mullin was born on June 15, 1970 in Daly City, California — a fourth-generation Californian. His father, Gene Mullin, served three terms in the California State Assembly, and his mother was a high school government teacher. Politics and civic engagement were central to the family's life from the start; as a senior in high school, Kevin participated in the Close-Up program in Washington, D.C.

He graduated from Junípero Serra High School in San Mateo in 1988, earned a B.A. in Communications from the University of San Francisco in 1992, and a Master of Public Administration from San Francisco State University in 1998. He also completed Harvard Kennedy School's Leadership for the 21st Century program. Mullin is hard of hearing and wears hearing aids — a personal experience that has shaped his legislative advocacy for the deaf and hard-of-hearing community, including a bill to create a Hearing Aid Assistance Tax Credit.

Before politics, Mullin founded KM2 Communications, a multimedia production company in South San Francisco, and worked as a television host and commentator on Peninsula-TV. He was also a mobile DJ known professionally as "Cutmaster Kevvy Kev."

California Political Career

Mullin served on the South San Francisco City Council from 2007 to 2012 and as Mayor of South San Francisco in 2011. He served as District Director for then-State Senator Jackie Speier before his own political career. In 2012, he was elected to the California State Assembly representing the 22nd District (San Mateo County). He served ten years in the Assembly, with over 60 bills signed into law. He served as Speaker pro Tempore from 2014 to 2022 — the longest tenure in the modern era — acting as the #2 officer of the Assembly, presiding over floor sessions and ruling on parliamentary inquiries.

His landmark legislation in the Assembly included authoring the DISCLOSE Act (campaign finance transparency), the Social Media DISCLOSE Act (disclosing who pays for political ads on social media), and championing all-vote-by-mail elections.

Congressional Career

Following Rep. Jackie Speier's retirement, Mullin ran for and won her CA-15 seat in 2022, defeating San Mateo County Supervisor David Canepa with 55.8% of the vote. He has served since January 2023. He serves on the House Energy and Commerce Committee (Commerce, Manufacturing, and Trade Subcommittee) and is a member of the New Democrat Coalition and Congressional Progressive Caucus.

In February 2025, Mullin left a California hospital bed with an IV still attached to fly to Washington and vote against a Trump-backed budget resolution. Democratic leadership had secretly arranged for several members with medical issues to return for the critical vote, which ultimately passed by the narrowest possible margin.

Key Votes

Dedication · February 2025
Left Hospital Bed with IV Attached to Vote Against Trump Budget Resolution
On February 25, 2025, Mullin flew to Washington from a California hospital with an IV still attached to his arm to vote against a Republican budget resolution backed by President Trump. Democratic leadership had secretly arranged for several hospitalized members to return for the vote. The resolution passed by the narrowest possible margin despite the Democrats' efforts.
AI / Tech · 2026
Introduced CHATBOT Act — Banning AI from Impersonating Licensed Professionals
Mullin introduced legislation to prevent AI chatbots from claiming to be licensed professionals such as doctors, lawyers, or therapists — citing cases where users had been harmed by chatbots providing professional advice without appropriate credentials.
Dark Money · 2026
Reintroduced Bill to End Dark Money in American Elections
In March 2026, Mullin reintroduced legislation to increase transparency in campaign financing by requiring disclosure of donors behind political advertisements — building on his authorship of California's DISCLOSE Act during his Assembly tenure.
State Legislation · Landmark
Authored California DISCLOSE Act & Social Media DISCLOSE Act
As a California Assemblymember, Mullin authored the DISCLOSE Act — landmark campaign finance transparency legislation requiring that the top donors to political ads be clearly identified. He also authored the Social Media DISCLOSE Act, extending those requirements to political advertising on social media platforms.
