BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · GovTrack · Ballotpedia · Schweikert.house.gov

David Sheridan Schweikert was born March 3, 1962. He earned a BS in Finance and Real Estate from Arizona State University. Before entering Congress he served in the Arizona State House (1991–1994), chaired the Arizona State Board of Equalization (1995–2004), and served as Maricopa County Treasurer (2004–2007). He worked professionally in real estate. He was first elected to Congress in 2010 and has represented Arizona's Scottsdale and North Phoenix area since. He is serving his eighth term.

House Ethics reprimand (2020): The House Ethics Committee investigated Schweikert starting in 2018 for using impermissible expenditures from his Members' Representational Allowance, receiving improper campaign contributions, and failure to make required financial disclosures. On July 30, 2020, the Committee found him guilty of all charges and recommended a formal reprimand and a $50,000 fine. The full House voted unanimously to formally reprimand him on July 31, 2020. In December 2021, his campaign committee agreed to pay a $125,000 fine to the FEC as part of a separate conciliation agreement. Source: GovTrack · Ballotpedia

Gay-baiting lawsuit (2022): Schweikert's 2022 campaign ran ads that implied Republican primary opponent Elijah Norton was gay. Norton sued the campaign and political consultants. The case settled in late 2023 and 2024, with political consultant Jonathan Huey releasing a statement: "As part of the settlement...I am hereby acknowledging the attacks were false." Source: Ballotpedia

Governor's race (2026): In September 2025, Schweikert announced he would not seek re-election to the House and would instead run for Arizona governor. His opponents in the Republican primary include Andy Biggs and Karrin Taylor Robson. He stated: "I've grown to believe Washington...is unsaveable. I do believe Arizona is savable." Source: Wikipedia

Legislative focus: Schweikert serves on the Ways & Means Committee and chairs the Joint Economic Committee. He has made the national debt a centerpiece of his work — his official website features a live debt tracker. He was instrumental in authoring and passing the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017. Source: Schweikert.house.gov
