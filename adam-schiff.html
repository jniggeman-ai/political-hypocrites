Background

Early Life & Education

Adam Schiff was born on June 22, 1960 in Framingham, Massachusetts. He attended Stanford University, graduating with a B.A. in 1982, and then earned his J.D. from Harvard Law School.

Legal Career

After law school, Schiff worked as a federal prosecutor in the U.S. Attorney's Office in Los Angeles. He prosecuted Richard Miller, an FBI agent who became the first FBI agent ever convicted of spying for the Soviet Union — a landmark case at the time.

Political Career

Schiff was elected to the California State Senate in 1996, serving until 2000. He was elected to the U.S. House of Representatives in 2000, representing a Pasadena-area district, and served 12 terms. He served as Chair of the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence.

Schiff was selected as lead impeachment manager for President Trump's first impeachment trial in 2020, related to the Ukraine phone call. He was also a member of the House Select Committee investigating the January 6, 2021 Capitol attack.

He ran for and won California's open U.S. Senate seat in 2024, defeating Republican Steve Garvey in the general election. He was sworn in on December 9, 2024, replacing the late Dianne Feinstein's seat.

Notable Controversies

House Action · June 21, 2023
Censured by the House of Representatives
The House voted 213–209 to censure Schiff for statements he made between 2017 and 2019 regarding evidence of Trump-Russia collusion. The resolution accused him of having "mislead the American public" in his role as Intelligence Committee ranking member and chairman. Schiff denied the charge and called the censure politically motivated. He is one of a small number of members to be censured by the House.
AIPAC Spending · 2024 Senate Primary
$5 Million in AIPAC-Affiliated Spending on His Behalf
The United Democracy Project, a super PAC affiliated with AIPAC (American Israel Public Affairs Committee), spent approximately $5 million supporting Schiff in his 2024 California Senate primary, helping him secure the top-two spot against a crowded field that included progressive Rep. Katie Porter.
Presidential Pardon · January 20, 2025
Pardoned by President Biden on Last Day in Office
On his final day as president, January 20, 2025, Joe Biden issued pardons to several prominent Democratic figures, including Schiff. Biden said the pardons were preemptive protections against potential politically motivated prosecutions by the incoming administration. Schiff had not been charged with any crime.

Key Votes

War Powers · 2003
Voted for the Iraq War Authorization
Schiff voted in favor of the Authorization for Use of Military Force Against Iraq in October 2002/2003. He later stated he "absolutely" regretted that vote, calling it a mistake.
Impeachment · 2020
Lead Manager for Trump First Impeachment
Schiff served as the lead House manager prosecuting Trump's first impeachment trial in the Senate in 2020, related to the Ukraine phone call and allegations of abuse of power and obstruction of Congress. The Senate voted to acquit.
Surveillance · 2023
FISA Section 702 Reauthorization
Schiff's positions on surveillance authorities have evolved over time. As Intelligence Committee Chair he generally supported intelligence community equities, though he also supported some reform measures.
