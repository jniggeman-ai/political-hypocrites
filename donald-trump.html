War, Military & Use of Force RecordSource: Federal Register · DOD · Congress.gov
Iran — Assassination of Qasem Soleimani (2020)Ordered Strike

Trump ordered the drone assassination of Iranian General Qasem Soleimani at Baghdad International Airport on January 3, 2020 — without congressional authorization. The strike killed Soleimani and nine others. Iran responded with ballistic missile strikes on U.S. bases in Iraq, injuring over 100 American service members.

⚠️ No congressional authorization was sought or obtained. The Senate passed a war powers resolution limiting Trump's ability to take further military action against Iran without congressional approval. Trump vetoed it. Iran had not attacked the United States directly before the assassination.

Iran War — 2025/2026Ongoing Conflict

Under Trump's second term, the U.S. entered direct military conflict with Iran. As of March 2026, U.S. service members have died in the conflict. Pete Hegseth has called Iran's new supreme leader "wounded and likely disfigured."

⚠️ No formal declaration of war was passed by Congress. Iran had not attacked U.S. soil. Multiple U.S. service members have been killed.

Syria — Tomahawk Missile Strike (2017)Ordered Strike

Ordered 59 Tomahawk cruise missile strikes against a Syrian air base following a chemical weapons attack by the Assad government. No congressional authorization was obtained.

⚠️ Syria had not attacked the United States. No congressional authorization was obtained.

Greenland — Threatened Military AcquisitionThreatened Force

Repeatedly stated his intention to acquire Greenland and refused to rule out military force when asked directly. Secretary of State Rubio echoed these statements in congressional testimony.

⚠️ Greenland is an autonomous territory of Denmark — a founding NATO ally. Threatening a NATO member with military force violates the foundational principles of the alliance the U.S. created.

January 6, 2021 — Capitol AttackIncited

Following his speech on January 6, 2021, a mob of his supporters stormed the U.S. Capitol in an attempt to prevent the certification of the 2020 election results. Trump was impeached by the House for "incitement of insurrection." He was acquitted by the Senate 57–43 — a majority voted to convict but fell short of the two-thirds threshold.

⚠️ 7 people died in connection with the attack. Over 1,200 people were charged with crimes. Trump was acquitted in his second impeachment trial but 57 of 100 senators — including many Republicans — voted that he was guilty.
