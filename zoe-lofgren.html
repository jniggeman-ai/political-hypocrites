Background

Early Life & Education

Zoe Lofgren was born in 1947 in San Mateo, California — the daughter of a beer truck driver who was a Teamsters union member and a cafeteria cook who later became a secretary. She worked the night shift at the Eastman Kodak plant in Palo Alto to save money for non-tuition college expenses before attending Stanford University on a California State Scholarship, graduating with a B.A. in political science in 1970. She earned her J.D. cum laude from Santa Clara University School of Law in 1975 on a scholarship. She has spent her entire life in the Bay Area.

Staffing Congress Through Nixon's Impeachment

After Stanford, Lofgren joined the staff of Congressman Don Edwards, serving eight years in both his San Jose and Washington D.C. offices. She was a member of the House Judiciary Committee staff in 1973 when the committee prepared articles of impeachment against President Richard Nixon — setting her on a path that would see her involved in three more presidential impeachment proceedings decades later.

San Mateo County & Congressional Career

Lofgren was elected to the San Mateo County Board of Supervisors in 1982, served ten years including as board president in 1986, and won the open Congressional seat in 1994, defeating San Jose's former mayor Tom McEnery in the Democratic primary by just over 1,100 votes. She has served continuously since January 1995, now in her CA-18 district covering much of San Jose and surrounding communities in Santa Clara, Monterey, Santa Cruz, and San Benito counties. She serves as Ranking Member of the House Science, Space and Technology Committee and chairs the California Democratic Congressional Delegation.

Notable Context

Campaign Finance · 2004–2008
Campaign Paid ~$350,000 to Husband's Businesses
During the 2004, 2006, and 2008 election cycles, Lofgren's campaign paid approximately $350,000 to two businesses operated by her husband: Collins and Day and John Marshall Collins P.C. The payments were for legal and consulting services. Such spousal payments are legal and disclosed, but attract scrutiny regarding conflicts of interest.
Decorum · May 2016
Publicly Reprimanded During Judiciary Hearing for Calling Witness an "Ignorant Bigot"
During a May 2016 House Judiciary Committee hearing, Lofgren was publicly reprimanded by acting chairman Steve King after she called witness Gail Heriot of the U.S. Commission on Civil Rights an "ignorant bigot" — a reference to Heriot's written testimony questioning whether gender self-identification makes someone a woman. Lofgren responded, "I cannot allow that kind of bigotry to go into the record unchallenged."

Key Votes

Impeachment · 2020 & 2021
Impeachment Manager — Trump's First and Second Trials
Lofgren was selected as one of seven House impeachment managers who presented the case against Trump in his first Senate trial (January 2020) — her third time in a presidential impeachment proceeding after staffing Nixon's in 1973 and managing Clinton's in 1999. She also served during Trump's second trial.
Jan 6 Committee
Member, House Select Committee on January 6
Lofgren served on the House Select Committee investigating the January 6, 2021 attack on the Capitol. The committee released its final report in December 2022.
Privacy · 2026
Introduced Comprehensive Federal Privacy Legislation
In March 2026, Lofgren introduced H.R. 8014 to establish individual rights over personal information and create a Digital Privacy Agency to enforce those rights — among the most comprehensive federal privacy bills introduced in the 119th Congress.

War & Foreign Policy

Science Committee
Ranking Member — Science, Space and Technology
As Ranking Member of the Science Committee, Lofgren focuses on research funding, STEM education, agricultural research, and technology policy — including the space program and federal investment in scientific infrastructure.
Immigration
Leading House Voice on Immigration Policy
Lofgren has been one of the House's most prominent immigration policy voices for decades, serving as lead Democrat on the Judiciary Subcommittee on Immigration. She was first to call for federal investigation of the Secure Communities deportation program in 2011.
