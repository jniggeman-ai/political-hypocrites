BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · Begich.house.gov · GovTrack · Ballotpedia

Nicholas Joseph Begich III was born October 21, 1977 in Anchorage, Alaska. He was raised by his maternal grandparents after his parents divorced. He attended The Master's Academy, a Florida high school, and later graduated from Baylor University with a BBA in Entrepreneurship and Indiana University Bloomington with an MBA. After working in product development and finance for Ford Motor Company, he returned to Alaska and founded FarShore Partners, an international software development company with 160 employees, primarily based in India. He later co-founded Dashfire, a startup investment firm.

Political family: Begich comes from a prominent Alaska political family. His grandfather, Nick Begich Sr., was Alaska's congressman who disappeared in a plane crash in 1972. His uncle, Mark Begich, served as a U.S. Senator from Alaska (2009–2015). His uncle, Tom Begich, served as Minority Leader of the Alaska Senate. He is the sixth person to hold Alaska's at-large House seat since statehood.

Election history: Begich ran three times for Alaska's House seat. In the August 2022 special election he was eliminated in the first round of ranked-choice voting despite being preferred by a majority of voters to each of his opponents — a result critics attributed to the ranked-choice system's "center squeeze" effect. He ran again in November 2022 but lost to Democrat Mary Peltola in the final RCV round. In 2024, with the primary field narrowed to two main candidates after Republican Nancy Dahlstrom withdrew, he defeated Peltola 51.3%–48.7% in the final round. He was sworn in January 3, 2025.

Legislative record (119th Congress): Begich became the first freshman member of the 119th Congress to have a bill passed — the Alaska Native Vietnam Era Veterans Land Allotment Extension Act (H.R. 410), which restored land rights to Alaska Native village corporations, passed nearly unanimously with bipartisan support in January 2025. He also passed legislation making it easier for disabled Alaska Natives to qualify for federal aid. He is a member of the Freedom Caucus.

One Big Beautiful Bill: Begich voted for the Trump-backed reconciliation bill and secured within it mandated oil and gas lease sales in the Coastal Plain of the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge (ANWR), the National Petroleum Reserve-Alaska, and Cook Inlet. The bill also increases Alaska's share of federal oil leasing revenues. Source: Wikipedia
