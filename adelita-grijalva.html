BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · PBS · Roll Call · AZ Mirror · AZ Luminaria

Adelita S. Grijalva was born October 30, 1970. She is a lifelong Tucsonan, graduating from Pueblo High School and earning a degree from the University of Arizona. Her professional experience includes serving as director of Pima County Teen Court, a diversion program for teens. She served on the Tucson Unified School District Governing Board from 2002 to 2022 — 20 years in education governance. She served on the Pima County Board of Supervisors for District 5 from 2021 to 2025, becoming the first Latina to serve as chair of the Board. She resigned her seat in 2025 to run for Congress.

Her father — Raúl Grijalva: Adelita is the daughter of Rep. Raúl Grijalva, who represented AZ-7 for more than 20 years before his death. Raúl Grijalva was diagnosed with lung cancer in April 2024 and died on March 13, 2025 from complications related to his cancer treatment at age 77. He was a founding co-chair of the Congressional Progressive Caucus and one of the most prominent progressive voices in the House. Source: Wikipedia

Special election (September 23, 2025): Adelita won the Democratic primary on July 15, 2025, defeating Deja Foxx, Daniel Hernández Jr., and others. She won the special general election by a 2-to-1 margin — approximately 71% — over Republican Daniel Butierez, the same candidate her father had defeated the previous November. She overperformed Kamala Harris's 22% margin in the district by a remarkable 40 points. Source: Wikipedia · Roll Call

Speaker Johnson's swearing-in delay: Johnson refused to swear Grijalva in after her election, citing the ongoing government shutdown as justification. The delay lasted 50 days — a new record for special election winners. Previously the record was 35 days, set by Rep. Jimmy Gomez in 2017 (who had requested the delay due to family issues). Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes and Grijalva filed a lawsuit to force Johnson's hand. Johnson was also accused of blocking her in part to prevent her from signing Rep. Thomas Massie's Epstein discharge petition, which needed one more signature to force a floor vote. Source: AZ Mirror · PBS · Wikipedia

Epstein pledge: On election night, Grijalva pledged from the stage that her first act in Congress would be to sign Massie's Epstein files discharge petition as the 218th and decisive signature. She invited two Epstein survivors to sit in the House gallery at her swearing-in. Source: KJZZ · Wikipedia

Epstein Files — Special NoteSource: PBS · KJZZ · Wikipedia · DocumentCloud

Epstein discharge petition pledge: On the night she won her special election, Adelita Grijalva pledged from the stage that her first act in Congress would be to sign Rep. Thomas Massie's discharge petition — which would force a floor vote on the Epstein Files Transparency Act. Massie was one signature away from the 218 needed. Grijalva stated she would be that 218th and decisive signature.

Swearing-in blocked: Speaker Mike Johnson's 50-day delay in swearing her in was widely criticized — including by Arizona AG Kris Mayes — in part because it prevented Grijalva from signing the petition and triggering the vote while the House was in session. Johnson denied the delay was connected to the Epstein petition. Source: PBS

Survivors at swearing-in: When Grijalva was finally sworn in on November 12, 2025, she had invited two Epstein survivors — Elizabeth Stein and Jessica Michaels — to sit in the House gallery as her guests. Source: Wikipedia
