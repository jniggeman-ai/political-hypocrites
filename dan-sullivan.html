BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · Sullivan.senate.gov · Britannica · Ballotpedia

Daniel Scott Sullivan was born November 13, 1964 in Fairview Park, Ohio. His father is Thomas C. Sullivan, President and CEO of RPM International, a major industrial coatings company. Sullivan attended Culver Military Academy in Indiana, then Harvard University (BA Economics, 1987), and Georgetown University Law Center (joint JD and Master of Science in Foreign Service, 1993).

Military service: Sullivan joined the U.S. Marine Corps in 1993 and served on active duty until 1997, then continued in the Marine Corps Reserve. He was recalled to active duty from 2004 to 2006, in 2009, and for a six-week tour in Afghanistan in 2013. In 1996 he deployed to the Taiwan Strait as part of the U.S. response to Chinese provocations during Taiwan's presidential elections. He retired as a Colonel in the Marine Corps Reserve on February 1, 2024, after 30 years of service. He is a recipient of the Defense Meritorious Service Medal.

Pre-Senate career: After leaving active duty, Sullivan clerked for federal judges in the Ninth Circuit and Alaska Supreme Court, then joined the Anchorage law firm Perkins Coie. He moved to Washington, D.C. to work in the Bush administration — first on the National Security Council and National Economic Council staffs, then as Assistant Secretary of State for Economic, Energy, and Business Affairs (2006–2009). In 2009, Alaska Governor Sarah Palin appointed him Alaska Attorney General. He later served as Commissioner of the Alaska Department of Natural Resources under Governor Sean Parnell.

Residency questions (2014): During his first Senate campaign, questions were raised about whether Sullivan was truly an Alaskan, as he had spent much of his career in Washington, D.C. and Ohio. His wife, a member of a prominent Fairbanks family, was featured in campaign ads to counter the issue. Source: Britannica

Climate science: Sullivan rejects the scientific consensus on climate change, stating "the verdict is still out on the human contribution to climate change." The scientific consensus is that human activity is a primary driver. Source: Wikipedia

War & Foreign Policy RecordSource: Wikipedia · Sullivan.senate.gov · Britannica

Taiwan Strait (1996): Sullivan deployed to the Taiwan Strait as a Marine infantry officer, as part of the U.S. response to Chinese military provocations during Taiwan's presidential election. This deployment shaped his lifelong focus on China as a strategic competitor.

Afghanistan: Sullivan completed a six-week active duty tour in Afghanistan in 2013 — one of three times he was recalled to active duty after leaving the regular Marine Corps.

Senate Armed Services Committee: Sullivan sits on the Senate Armed Services Committee and has been a consistent voice for military readiness, missile defense, and countering China and North Korea in the Pacific. He supported the Tommy Tuberville military promotion hold critics, publicly condemning the blockade as damaging to national security.

China: Sullivan has a documented record of hawkish positions on China, dating to his Taiwan Strait deployment. He introduced legislation and resolutions opposing Chinese Communist Party activities. He chaired the International Republican Institute, which promotes democracy internationally.

North Korea / Missile Defense: Sullivan has been a consistent advocate for U.S. missile defense systems, citing North Korea's ICBM program as a direct threat to Alaska and Hawaii. Source: Wikipedia
