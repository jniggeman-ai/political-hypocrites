BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · Encyclopedia of Arkansas · Hill.house.gov · GovTrack

James French Hill was born December 5, 1956 in Little Rock, Arkansas. He is a ninth-generation Arkansan. His father Jay F. Hill ran a Little Rock-based financial firm inherited from his own father. As a teenager, Hill worked in the family financial firm during summer months. He graduated magna cum laude in economics from Vanderbilt University in 1979 and became a certified corporate director through a UCLA program. He is a Catholic and identifies as Arkansas Creole — a cultural heritage of mixed French, Spanish, African, and Native American ancestry in the Louisiana-Arkansas region.

Government service: From 1982 to 1984, Hill was an aide to Republican Senator John Tower on the Senate Banking Committee. He served as Deputy Assistant Secretary of the Treasury for Corporate Finance under President George H.W. Bush from 1989 to 1991, where he represented the U.S. in bilateral talks with Japan (the Structural Impediments Initiative) and led U.S. technical assistance to emerging economies in Eastern Europe after the fall of the Berlin Wall. He was senior policy adviser to Bush's Economic Policy Council 1991–1993. Source: Wikipedia · Encyclopedia of Arkansas

Banking career: From 1993 to 1998 he was executive officer of First Commercial Corporation. In 1999 he founded Delta Trust & Banking Corporation in Little Rock, serving as chairman and CEO until its acquisition by Simmons First National Bank in 2014 — at which point he ran for Congress. Source: Wikipedia

Financial Services Committee: Hill was named chair of the House Financial Services Committee in December 2024 — one of the most powerful committee assignments in Congress, with jurisdiction over banking, financial markets, housing, and monetary policy. He shepherded the FIT21 digital assets regulatory framework (the "Clarity Act") through the House with 76 Democratic votes. He also sponsored a 2025 bill to rescind the CFPB rule capping bank overdraft fees at $5. Source: Hill.house.gov · Wikipedia
