BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · Ballotpedia · GovTrack · OpenSecrets

Gary James Palmer was born May 14, 1954 in Hackleburg, Alabama, where his family farmed 40 acres. He was the first in his family to earn a college degree, receiving a BS in Operations Management from the University of Alabama, where he was also a walk-on wide receiver for Bear Bryant's football team.

Alabama Policy Institute: Before entering elected office Palmer co-founded and served as president of the Alabama Policy Institute (API), a conservative think tank, for 24 years. His involvement with Focus on the Family led him to start the Alabama Family Alliance, which became API. He chaired the Republican Policy Committee from 2019 to 2025 — the fourth-ranking position in House Republican leadership — making him the most senior Republican policy voice from Alabama.

Election history: Palmer won the 2014 Republican primary in a heavily contested seven-way field, ultimately defeating state Rep. Paul DeMarco 64%–36% after picking up Club for Growth support. He won the general election 76%–24% in the R+28 district. He has held the seat since. His district includes the wealthier parts of Birmingham and most of its suburbs.

Term limits pledge: Palmer pledged before his first election to serve no more than five terms. The 2022 election would have been his last under that pledge. In March 2022 he reversed course, citing his leadership role and high turnover in the Alabama delegation as justification. The U.S. Term Limits group purchased billboards attacking him for breaking the pledge. His office called the accusation "fake news." Source: Wikipedia

Infrastructure vote: In November 2021, Palmer touted federal funding for the Birmingham Northern Beltline — a provision he authored in the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act — while simultaneously voting against the final bill. He did not mention his no vote in a Twitter post celebrating the funding. Democratic figures including Sen. Brian Schatz and Rep. Eric Swalwell criticized the post. Source: Wikipedia

FTX donations: In February 2023, CoinDesk reported Palmer was one of three Alabama delegation members who received donations from FTX — the collapsed cryptocurrency exchange whose founder Sam Bankman-Fried was later convicted of fraud. Palmer's office did not respond to inquiries about what was done with the funds. Source: CoinDesk · Wikipedia

Speaker candidacy (2023): During the October 2023 House Speaker election, Palmer briefly entered the race as a potential unity candidate. The New York Times described him as someone who could bridge the warring Republican factions. He withdrew before an internal caucus forum. Source: Wikipedia
