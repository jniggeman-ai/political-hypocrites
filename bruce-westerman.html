BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · Encyclopedia of Arkansas · House History · GovTrack

Bruce Eugene Westerman was born November 18, 1967 in Hot Springs, Garland County, Arkansas. He graduated as valedictorian of Fountain Lake High School. He attended the University of Arkansas in Fayetteville, where he played college football for the Razorbacks as a walk-on, graduating with a BS in Biological and Agricultural Engineering in 1990. He returned to earn an MS in Forestry from Yale University in 2001. He worked as an engineer and forester for 22 years at Mid-South Engineering in Hot Springs and was named Engineer of the Year by the Arkansas Society of Professional Engineers in 2013.

State legislature: Westerman was elected to the Arkansas House of Representatives in 2010. He served as House Minority Leader in 2012 and Majority Leader in 2013 — becoming the first Republican House Majority Leader in Arkansas since Reconstruction after the GOP took control of the chamber. He was the only forester in the state legislature. Source: House History

January 6, 2021: Westerman signed an amicus brief in December 2020 seeking to void the presidential election results in four Democratic-leaning states. During the January 6 Capitol riot, he was left behind in Kevin McCarthy's office when security evacuated the minority leader. As the threat grew closer, Westerman took a Civil War sword from a shattered display case for protection and hid on a toilet to avoid the rioters. He voted to certify the election results after the attack. He has since voted against both a bipartisan commission and a select committee to investigate January 6. Source: Wikipedia · Encyclopedia of Arkansas

Natural Resources Committee: Westerman has chaired the House Natural Resources Committee since 2023. The committee has jurisdiction over public lands, national parks, energy, mining, water, and Native American affairs. He has a lifetime score of 4% from the League of Conservation Voters — near the lowest possible — and introduced the controversial Trillion Trees Act in 2021, which scientists and environmental groups criticized. He also introduced the Fix Our Forests Act in January 2025 alongside a California Democrat, focusing on wildfire risk reduction. Source: Wikipedia
