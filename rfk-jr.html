Who Is RFK Jr.?

Robert F. Kennedy Jr. is the son of the late Senator Robert F. Kennedy and the nephew of President John F. Kennedy — one of the most famous political family names in American history. He built his early career as an environmental lawyer, winning significant pollution cases against corporations. He was widely respected in those circles.

His transformation into America's most prominent anti-vaccine activist began in the early 2000s. He founded Children's Health Defense — a nonprofit that became the most influential anti-vaccine organization in the United States, raising tens of millions of dollars annually while spreading claims that vaccines cause autism, a claim comprehensively and repeatedly debunked by decades of peer-reviewed science across dozens of countries.

In 2023 he ran for president — first as a Democrat, then as an independent. He dropped out in August 2024 and endorsed Donald Trump, who subsequently appointed him to lead the $1.8 trillion Department of Health and Human Services — the agency that oversees the CDC, FDA, and NIH, and that sets U.S. vaccine policy.

His own family publicly opposed his HHS nomination. His sister Kerry Kennedy and cousin Caroline Kennedy both expressed alarm. His sister Kerry called it "a danger to public health."

Actions Taken as HHS SecretarySource: NPR · PBS · KFF Health News · Rolling Stone · APHA
Clawed Back $11 Billion in COVID-Era Vaccine GrantsConfirmed

Weeks after taking office — and after pledging during confirmation hearings not to undermine vaccine funding — the CDC under Kennedy's direction pulled back $11 billion in COVID-era grants that local health departments were using to fund vaccination programs. A federal judge later ordered HHS to distribute the money. Source: KFF Health News · NPR

Cancelled $500M in mRNA Vaccine ResearchConfirmed

Ordered the termination of approximately $500 million in NIH contracts to develop vaccines using mRNA technology — the same technology used in the highly effective COVID-19 vaccines. No scientific basis was cited. Source: PBS · KFF Health News

Eliminated 20,000 HHS JobsConfirmed

Oversaw a sweeping restructuring of HHS that shut down entire agencies, consolidated others, and laid off approximately 10,000 employees on top of 10,000 others who had already taken buyouts. Georgetown University public health law professor Lawrence Gostin called it a hollowing out of U.S. scientific leadership. Source: PBS NewHour

Funded Controversial $1.6M Vaccine Study With "Questionable Research Practices"Documented

Directed $1.6 million to two Danish researchers — Peter Aaby and Christine Stabell Benn — just days after a group of top Danish statisticians documented "questionable research practices" in their work. The study withheld hepatitis B vaccines from half of 14,000 newborns in Guinea-Bissau. Dr. Paul Offit compared it to the Tuskegee experiment. Source: Rolling Stone

Promoted Discredited Autism-Vaccine Link on CDC WebsiteConfirmed

While technically keeping the CDC's statement that vaccines do not cause autism, Kennedy allowed new statements to be added to the same webpage baselessly casting doubt on vaccine safety — and claiming the public has largely ignored studies showing vaccines do cause autism. That statement is false. Source: KFF Health News · NPR

Fired or Forced Out FDA Vaccine Chief and Multiple NIH DirectorsConfirmed

Forced out Dr. Peter Marks — the FDA's leading vaccine official who led Operation Warp Speed. Marks wrote in his resignation letter: "It has become clear that truth and transparency are not desired by the secretary, but rather he wishes subservient confirmation of his misinformation and lies." Also fired four NIH directors and a CDC director he had hired less than a month earlier. Source: APHA · PBS

Changed Federal Position on Fluoride, Seed Oils, and TylenolPolicy Action

Announced plans to tell the CDC to stop recommending fluoridation of community water. Promoted discredited links between Tylenol and autism. Changed government positions on seed oils. These moves were praised by MAHA supporters and criticized by mainstream public health experts. Source: PBS

Kennedy's Health Claims — What The Research Shows Source: CDC · WHO · NIH · peer-reviewed studies · FDA

This tab presents Kennedy's positions alongside the current state of the published research. Science evolves. Where genuine debate exists in the literature, that is noted. Where there is broad consensus, that is noted. Visitors are encouraged to read the primary sources themselves.

Kennedy's PositionState of the ResearchWhere Things Stand
MMR vaccine causes autism
Kennedy has long cited a link between the MMR vaccine and autism.		 The original 1998 Wakefield study that proposed this link was retracted by The Lancet after an investigation found data manipulation. Subsequent large-scale studies — including one of 1.2 million Danish children published in 2019 — have not found a causal link. However, Kennedy and others argue these studies have methodological limitations and that the question deserves continued independent research. Active Debate — Mainstream consensus finds no link. Independent researchers continue to call for further study.
Aluminum adjuvants in vaccines may cause harm
Kennedy has raised concerns about aluminum used in some vaccines.		 A 2024 study of 1.2 million Danish children found no link between aluminum in vaccines and chronic diseases including autism. However, some researchers argue long-term systemic effects of aluminum adjuvants have not been fully studied. The FDA considers currently used levels safe based on available data. Ongoing Research — FDA considers current levels safe. Some independent researchers want more study.
Pharmaceutical industry has corrupted regulatory agencies
Kennedy has long alleged the FDA and CDC are compromised by industry funding and revolving door hiring.		 The revolving door between regulatory agencies and the pharmaceutical industry is extensively documented. Multiple peer-reviewed studies and government watchdog reports have documented conflicts of interest at the FDA and CDC. This concern is shared by researchers across the political spectrum and is not disputed as a systemic issue — the degree of its impact on decisions is debated. Broadly Supported — Regulatory capture and conflicts of interest at federal health agencies are well documented.
Ultra-processed food is driving chronic disease
Central to his MAHA platform.		 A growing body of peer-reviewed research supports links between ultra-processed food consumption and increased rates of obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and other chronic conditions. This position is increasingly mainstream in nutritional science and is not substantially contested. Broadly Supported — Strong and growing body of evidence supports this position.
Environmental toxins contribute to the autism and chronic disease epidemic
Kennedy has pointed to pesticides, heavy metals, and other environmental exposures.		 Research into environmental contributors to neurodevelopmental disorders is an active and growing field. Studies have found associations between pesticide exposure (particularly organophosphates), heavy metals, and neurodevelopmental outcomes. The science here is genuinely unsettled and evolving — this is a legitimate area of research that many mainstream scientists are actively pursuing. Actively Researched — Associations found in some studies. Causation not fully established. Legitimate scientific inquiry.
Fluoride in water at current levels poses health risks
Kennedy moved to end the federal fluoridation recommendation.		 A 2024 review by the National Toxicology Program found that higher fluoride exposure is associated with lower IQ in children, though the levels studied were generally higher than U.S. tap water levels. The CDC, WHO, and American Dental Association maintain the current recommendation. Some scientists argue existing safety thresholds need revisiting in light of new data. This is a genuinely emerging debate. Emerging Debate — New NTP research has added complexity. Most health bodies still support current levels. Some scientists call for review.
The COVID-19 mRNA vaccines caused widespread harm
Kennedy cancelled $500M in mRNA research and has questioned mRNA vaccine safety.		 Post-market surveillance has documented rare but real adverse events from mRNA COVID vaccines — including myocarditis in young males (primarily mild and self-resolving) and rare cases of thrombosis. Major health bodies maintain the benefits outweighed these risks for most populations. Some researchers argue the risk-benefit calculation varied significantly by age group and prior immunity and that this was not adequately communicated. Independent researchers continue to analyze the data. Nuanced — Rare adverse events are documented and real. Debate continues over whether risk-benefit communication was adequate, particularly for certain age groups.
COVID-19 was engineered as a bioweapon targeting specific ethnic groups
Kennedy made this claim in 2023.		 No peer-reviewed scientific evidence supports the claim that COVID-19 was engineered to target specific ethnic groups. The virus's genetic structure does not support ethnic targeting. The broader question of COVID-19's origins — lab leak vs. natural spillover — remains officially unresolved, with U.S. intelligence agencies divided. The ethnic targeting component specifically has no scientific support. No Scientific Support — The ethnic targeting claim specifically has no basis in virology or genetics.
