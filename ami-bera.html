Background

Early Life & Education

Amerish Babulal "Ami" Bera was born on March 2, 1965 in Los Angeles, California. He is a lifelong Californian and the son of Indian immigrants — his father Babulal emigrated from Rajkot, Gujarat, India in 1958. Bera earned both his B.S. and his M.D. from the University of California, Irvine. He completed his residency in internal medicine at California Pacific Medical Center, where he became chief resident.

Medical Career

Bera practiced internal medicine in the Sacramento area and served in leadership roles at MedClinic Medical Group. He then worked as medical director of care management for Mercy Healthcare, overseeing a seven-hospital system. He subsequently served as Chief Medical Officer for Sacramento County, where he worked to reduce the cost of care and expand access to health services for tens of thousands of county residents.

Congressional Career

Bera was first elected to Congress in 2012, defeating three-term Republican incumbent Dan Lungren in a race that wasn't called until a week after election night. He is now the longest-serving Indian American member of Congress. His district, currently CA-6, covers suburban Sacramento County — including Citrus Heights, Rancho Cordova, Carmichael, and Fair Oaks.

Bera is a centrist. He is a member of the New Democrat Coalition and formerly co-chaired the bipartisan Problem Solvers Caucus. He serves on the House Foreign Affairs Committee as Ranking Member of the Subcommittee on East Asia and the Pacific, and on the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence. Due to redistricting under Proposition 50, Bera is running in the redrawn CA-3 in 2026.

Notable Controversies

Federal Crime · 2016
Father Convicted of Election Fraud — Sentenced to Federal Prison
In May 2016, Bera's father, Babulal Bera, pleaded guilty to two felony counts of federal election fraud. He was sentenced in August 2016 to one year and one day in federal prison and fined $100,200. According to federal prosecutors, Babulal orchestrated a scheme spanning the 2010 and 2012 election cycles in which he facilitated approximately 130 improper campaign contributions totaling over $260,000 — far exceeding the legal limit of $4,900 — by recruiting approximately 90 friends, family members, and acquaintances to make contributions and then reimbursing them with his own funds. The acting U.S. Attorney stated that neither Bera nor his campaign staff had been found to have knowledge of or participation in his father's activities, and closed the investigation without further charges. Bera stated he was unaware of his father's actions and expressed personal devastation at the conviction.
Bipartisanship · March 2025
One of Ten Democrats to Vote to Censure Rep. Al Green
On March 6, 2025, Bera was one of only ten House Democrats to join all Republicans in voting to censure Democratic Rep. Al Green of Texas, who had interrupted President Trump's address to a joint session of Congress. The move drew criticism from progressive Democrats who argued Green was expressing legitimate dissent.
Policy · November 2015
Voted to Effectively Halt Syrian Refugee Resettlement
In November 2015, Bera voted for H.R. 4038, legislation that would have imposed additional security screening requirements stringent enough to effectively pause the resettlement of Syrian and Iraqi refugees in the United States. The vote drew criticism from Democratic colleagues and immigrant rights organizations.

Intelligence Committee
Senior Democrat, House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence
Bera serves as a senior Democrat on the House Intelligence Committee, giving him access to classified intelligence briefings. He publicly questioned DNI Tulsi Gabbard in 2026 about whether intelligence assessments supported the administration's justification for strikes on Iran.

Ranking Member, Subcommittee on East Asia and the Pacific
As Ranking Member on the East Asia subcommittee, Bera has focused on Taiwan policy, U.S.-China relations, and THAAD redeployment. He has pressed Trump administration officials on U.S. commitments to Taiwan's security and raised concerns about military posture in the region.
