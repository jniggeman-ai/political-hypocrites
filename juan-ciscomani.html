BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · Ciscomani.house.gov · Ballotpedia · AZPM

Juan Ciscomani III was born August 31, 1982 in Hermosillo, Sonora, Mexico, and moved to Tucson with his family as a child. He attended Pima Community College and the University of Arizona, becoming the first member of his family to graduate from college — earning scholarships to do so. After graduating he worked at the University of Arizona as a program development specialist. He and his wife Laura have six children. He is Protestant.

Early career — interned for Democrats: In 2003, Ciscomani interned for U.S. Representative Ed Pastor, a Democrat from Arizona. He then completed a fellowship with Rep. Loretta Sanchez, another Democratic member of Congress. He later became a senior adviser to Republican Governor Doug Ducey and vice chair of the Arizona-Mexico Commission. Source: Wikipedia

2023 SOTU response: Ciscomani was selected to deliver the Republican response to the 2023 State of the Union Address — in Spanish — a recognition of his profile as a Latino Republican voice. Source: Wikipedia

Tossup district: AZ-6 is one of only 18 House districts nationally rated as tossups by the Cook Political Report heading into 2026. Ciscomani won in 2022 and 2024 by narrow margins. His 2024 victory over Democratic nominee Kirsten Engel was by under 11,000 votes. He sits on the House Appropriations Committee and its Homeland Security subcommittee — significant committee assignments for a two-term member. Source: AZPM · Ciscomani.house.gov

Medicaid / ACA: In May 2025, Ciscomani was among a group of House Republicans who wrote a letter stating they "cannot, and will not, support any legislation that reduces Medicaid benefits for vulnerable populations." He declined to say whether he supported full ACA repeal. He ultimately voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill, which included Medicaid work requirements — Rep. Ansari noted he "expressed displeasure with the final bill before folding and voting yes." Source: Wikipedia · AZPM
