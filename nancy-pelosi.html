Background

Early Life & Family

Nancy Patricia D'Alesandro was born on March 26, 1940 in Baltimore, Maryland, into a political dynasty. Her father, Thomas D'Alesandro Jr., represented Maryland in Congress for five terms before serving as Mayor of Baltimore for twelve years. Her brother, Thomas D'Alesandro III, later also served as Mayor of Baltimore. Pelosi grew up in the family's Little Italy rowhouse, where the local church and Democratic Party office were the twin centers of community life.

She earned a B.A. in political science from Trinity College in Washington, D.C. in 1962. She married Paul Pelosi in 1963 and the couple eventually settled in San Francisco, where she raised five children while building a career as a Democratic Party volunteer and fundraiser.

Pre-Congressional Career

Before running for office, Pelosi spent decades as a behind-the-scenes party operative. She served as Chair of the Northern California Democratic Party (1977–81), Chair of the California State Democratic Party (1981–83), host committee chair for the 1984 Democratic National Convention in San Francisco, and finance chair of the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee (1985–86). She was known as one of the party's most effective fundraisers before she ever held office.

Congressional Career

Pelosi entered Congress in 1987, winning a special election to fill the seat of Rep. Sala Burton, who had encouraged her to run before dying of cancer. She has represented San Francisco — one of the most Democratic districts in the country — ever since, under various district numbers (5th, 8th, 12th, 11th).

She became House Minority Whip in 2002, then Minority Leader — the first woman to lead a major party in Congress. In 2007, following the Democrats' 2006 midterm wave, she became Speaker of the House — the first and still only woman to hold the position. She passed the Affordable Care Act in 2010 with a three-seat majority, a legislative feat widely credited to her vote-counting skill. Democrats lost the House in 2010 and she became Minority Leader again until 2019, when Democrats retook the chamber and she reclaimed the speakership — the first person in more than 60 years to serve nonconsecutive terms as Speaker. She stepped down from leadership in November 2022 but continued to serve as a member. She is referred to as Speaker Emerita.

On November 6, 2025, Pelosi announced she would not seek re-election in 2026, ending a 40-year congressional career.

Top Donors & Funding

Pelosi raised more than $400 million in campaign funds on behalf of Democratic candidates and party committees between 2003 and 2014, making her one of the most prolific political fundraisers in congressional history.

Key Votes

Healthcare · 2010
Led Passage of the Affordable Care Act
As Speaker with only a three-seat Democratic majority, Pelosi shepherded the Affordable Care Act through the House on March 21, 2010 — extending coverage to approximately 30 million uninsured Americans and barring insurers from denying coverage for pre-existing conditions. The vote was 219–212. Congressional scholars credit the ACA's passage in large part to Pelosi's vote-counting and legislative management.
Impeachment · 2019 & 2021
Led Both Trump Impeachments
Pelosi launched the formal impeachment inquiry into President Trump in September 2019, leading to his first impeachment (abuse of power, obstruction of Congress) on December 18, 2019 — a vote of 230–197. She led the second impeachment on January 13, 2021 — ten days after the Capitol attack — on a charge of incitement of insurrection, with the House voting 232–197, including 10 Republicans. Both Senate trials resulted in acquittal.
Iraq War · 2002
Voted Against the Iraq War Authorization
Pelosi voted against the 2002 Authorization for Use of Military Force Against Iraq, which passed the House 296–133. She called the case for war insufficient and warned that unilateral military action would harm the broader war on terrorism.
Floor Record · February 2018
Set Record for Longest House Floor Speech — 8+ Hours
On February 7, 2018, Pelosi delivered an 8-hour-plus speech on the House floor — the longest in the chamber's recorded history — opposing a budget deal that lifted spending caps without addressing immigration protections for DACA recipients.

War & Foreign Policy

Iraq · 2002
Voted Against Iraq War Authorization
One of 133 House members to vote against the 2002 AUMF authorizing the use of force in Iraq. As a member of the House Intelligence Committee at the time, she said the intelligence did not justify the risk of unilateral military action.
Interrogation · 2002–2009
CIA Waterboarding Briefing Controversy
In 2002, while serving as Ranking Member of the House Intelligence Committee, Pelosi was briefed by the CIA on "enhanced interrogation techniques," including waterboarding, being used on captured terrorism suspect Abu Zubaydah. Pelosi later said she was told the techniques were legal and that she did not raise substantial objections at the time. In 2009, she stated she had been misled by CIA lawyers about the nature of the briefing. Two unnamed former Bush administration officials disputed this account and said the briefing had been detailed and that she did not object. The CIA's own director subsequently told Congress the agency had misled lawmakers over several years. The dispute remained unresolved publicly.
Taiwan · August 2022
Led Congressional Delegation to Taiwan — China Responded with Military Exercises
On August 2–3, 2022, Pelosi led a congressional delegation to Taiwan — the highest-ranking U.S. official to visit since Speaker Newt Gingrich in 1997. The visit was widely controversial given heightened U.S.-China tensions. China responded by imposing sanctions on Taiwan, conducting live-fire military exercises around the island for several days, and expanding its military presence in the Taiwan Strait. Pelosi reaffirmed U.S. support for Taiwanese democracy.

Stock Trades

Ongoing Scrutiny · Multi-Year
Husband Paul Pelosi's Trading Record — Timing Questions
Paul Pelosi, a venture capitalist, has executed stock trades that have drawn sustained public scrutiny due to their timing relative to congressional legislation and regulatory action. Notable examples include: purchasing over $1 million in Nvidia call options in 2022 shortly before a congressional vote on the CHIPS Act (which provided $52 billion in semiconductor subsidies); and selling over $500,000 in Visa stock a few months before the Department of Justice filed an antitrust lawsuit against Visa in 2024. Pelosi's office has consistently stated she "does not own any stocks" and has "no prior knowledge or subsequent involvement in any transactions," with disclosures marked "SP" for Spouse. No charges have been filed against Pelosi or her husband. A "Nancy Pelosi Stock Tracker" account on X amassed over 1.2 million followers, and an ETF (ticker: NANC) was launched to mirror the portfolio's reported trades. The New York Times reported in 2022 that no concrete evidence existed that Pelosi profited from insider knowledge, while also noting the portfolio significantly outperformed market benchmarks. During Trump's March 2025 State of the Union address, he singled Pelosi out by name while calling for passage of stock trading restrictions — drawing bipartisan applause from the floor.
