Background & RecordSource: Wikipedia · ODNI · NPR · Al Jazeera · OpenSecrets

Tulsi Gabbard was born on April 12, 1981 in American Samoa and grew up in Hawaii. She joined the Hawaii Army National Guard in 2003 and was deployed to Iraq in 2004–2005, where she served with a medical unit and received the Combat Medical Badge. She deployed to Kuwait in 2008 as a Military Police officer, and later completed a special operations mission to counter terrorism in Africa. She is currently a Lieutenant Colonel in the Army Reserve.

Political career: Gabbard was elected to the Hawaii State Legislature at age 21, making her the youngest woman elected to a state legislature in U.S. history at the time. She was elected to the U.S. House of Representatives in 2012, serving four terms (2013–2021) representing Hawaii's 2nd District. She made history as the first Samoan American and first Hindu American member of Congress. She served on the Armed Services, Homeland Security, and Foreign Affairs committees.

Political transformation: Gabbard was a Democrat for most of her career. She ran for the 2020 Democratic presidential nomination, where she notably attacked Kamala Harris in a debate moment that went viral. She endorsed Bernie Sanders and then Joe Biden. She left the Democratic Party in October 2022, declaring it had been captured by an "elitist cabal of warmongers." She became independent, then joined the Republican Party in 2024 and endorsed Trump.

Foreign policy controversies: Gabbard met privately with Syrian dictator Bashar al-Assad in 2017 — a meeting that drew widespread condemnation from Democrats and Republicans alike. She has repeatedly promoted positions on Syria and Russia that critics, including Mitt Romney, described as "parroting Russian propaganda." She spread false claims about U.S.-funded biological weapons labs in Ukraine — a claim Senator Romney publicly called "treasonous lies." At her confirmation hearing, senators repeatedly asked her to call Edward Snowden a traitor; she declined each time.

As DNI: Confirmed 52–48 — the narrowest margin of any Trump Cabinet member. She now oversees 18 intelligence agencies and a budget exceeding $100 billion. She is the first female combat veteran to hold the position. Mitch McConnell, who was Senate Republican leader for 18 years, cast the only Republican vote against her confirmation, calling her "an unnecessary risk." Every Democrat and independent also voted no.

PAC fundraising questions: OpenSecrets flagged Gabbard's post-2023 PAC operations, which raised millions but spent only a small fraction on candidates — all MAGA-aligned Republicans. The gap between fundraising and spending drew questions about financial management and transparency.

✅ On Record
Promised to depoliticize the intelligence community.
Stated this as her primary mission at confirmation. She pledged to "end the weaponization and politicization" of intelligence agencies and provide "unbiased, apolitical" analysis to the president. Source: ODNI · NPR
⚠️ Disputed
Promised her past foreign policy views would not affect her official duties.
Gabbard pledged to separate her personal views from her role as DNI. Critics including Senate Intelligence Committee Democrats argued her documented history of promoting Russian-aligned narratives made this assurance insufficient — particularly for someone overseeing the nation's most sensitive secrets. Source: Al Jazeera · NJ Monitor
⛔ Prior Record
Spread false claims about U.S.-funded bioweapons labs in Ukraine (2022).
Gabbard publicly claimed the U.S. was funding biological weapons laboratories in Ukraine — a claim that was false and directly echoed Russian disinformation narratives. Senator Mitt Romney publicly called it "treasonous lies" that "may well cost lives." She now oversees all U.S. intelligence collection on Russia and Ukraine. Source: Wikipedia · Romney statement
⛔ Prior Record
Met privately with Assad without informing Congress (2017).
Gabbard traveled to Syria and met privately with President Bashar al-Assad in January 2017 — a meeting she did not disclose to House leadership in advance. Assad's government has used chemical weapons against civilians, a fact confirmed by U.S. and international intelligence. She now oversees U.S. intelligence operations in Syria and the broader Middle East. Source: Wikipedia
