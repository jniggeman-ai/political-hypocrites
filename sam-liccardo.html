Background

Early Life & Heritage

Samuel Theodore Liccardo was born on April 16, 1970 in San Jose, California. He is one of five children of Salvador and Laura (née Aceves) Liccardo. He is of Sicilian and Irish descent, and also of Mexican Californio ancestry — tracing family roots to the early Californio inhabitants of the Bay Area and to more recent Mexican immigrants who worked in the Almaden mines. His paternal grandfather owned and operated the Notre Dame Market, a neighborhood grocery store in downtown San Jose. He grew up in San Jose and attended Bellarmine College Prep.

He earned a B.A. from Georgetown University, a Master of Public Policy from Harvard Kennedy School, and a J.D. from Harvard Law School. He also taught several courses at Stanford University on urban solutions to homelessness, crime, and climate change after leaving the mayor's office.

Prosecutorial Career

Before elected office, Liccardo was a criminal prosecutor in the Santa Clara County District Attorney's Office, where he prosecuted felony cases of sexual assault and child exploitation. He also served as a federal prosecutor in the Southern District of California, handling cases ranging from narcotics trafficking to crimes against children.

San Jose City Council (2006–2014)

Liccardo was elected to the San Jose City Council in 2006, representing downtown District 3, and served for eight years. On the council he championed an inclusionary zoning ordinance requiring developers to make 15% of units in any project affordable — a measure the homebuilding industry sued to block. The California Supreme Court ultimately sided with the city in 2015. He also advocated for high-rise housing in downtown San Jose, including a 23-story tower at One South Market.

Mayor of San José (2015–2023)

Liccardo was elected Mayor of San José in a competitive 2014 runoff, defeating County Supervisor Dave Cortese 50.8%–49.2%. He was re-elected in 2018 with 75.8% of the vote and was term-limited out in 2023. San Jose is the largest city in Northern California and the 10th largest in the United States.

As mayor, Liccardo positioned San José as the largest U.S. city to commit to carbon neutrality, launching San José Clean Energy which now procures 95% of electricity from renewable sources. He led regional efforts to expand BART and helped electrify Caltrain. He helped rebuild the San Jose Police Department through two ballot measures that added over 200 officers over four years. In his final year in office, San Jose had the lowest homicide rate among the 50 largest U.S. cities.

On housing and homelessness — which he has publicly called his biggest failure as mayor — Liccardo pioneered motel-to-housing conversions (a model California later adopted statewide), prefabricated quick-build housing communities, and a "Cash for Trash" program employing unhoused residents. Despite these efforts, homelessness continued to rise throughout most of his tenure; the city achieved an 11% reduction in street homelessness only in his final year, 2022. He has publicly acknowledged failing to reach his housing production goals, citing construction costs, the COVID-19 pandemic, and the failure of a $450 million housing bond measure.

On gun policy, Liccardo enacted what he described as the country's most ambitious gun ordinance — a first-in-the-nation requirement for gun owners to pay annual fees to fund violence-prevention programs and to purchase liability insurance. Critics argued the ordinance was unenforceable and largely symbolic.

Congressional Career

Liccardo ran for the open CA-16 seat in 2024 following Anna Eshoo's retirement, winning a crowded primary and then defeating fellow Democrat Evan Low in the general election. He took office January 3, 2025, serving on the House Financial Services Committee. He is Chair of the New Democrat Coalition's Innovation & Technology Working Group, and in July 2025 spearheaded the New Dems' first-ever Innovation Agenda.

Five of his housing bills were included in the Senate-passed 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act.

Key Votes

Housing · 2025–2026
Five Housing Bills Passed in Senate Package
Liccardo authored five housing bills that were included in the Senate-passed 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, covering office-to-housing conversions, housing supply streamlining, and related affordability measures — applying lessons from his mayoral experience to federal policy.
Innovation · July 2025
Spearheaded New Dem Coalition's First-Ever Innovation Agenda
As Chair of the New Democrat Coalition's Innovation & Technology Working Group, Liccardo led development of the New Dems' first-ever Innovation Agenda in July 2025 — a policy framework focused on AI, semiconductors, broadband, and clean technology competitiveness.
Bipartisan · 2026
Introduced Keep Innovators in America Act to Retain U.S.-Trained Talent
Liccardo introduced the bipartisan Keep Innovators in America Act with Republicans Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-IL), codifying the Optional Practical Training (OPT) program to allow U.S.-trained foreign students and workers to remain in the country and contribute to the innovation economy.
