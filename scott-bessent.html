Background & RecordSource: Wikipedia · Treasury · Britannica · Miller Center · OpenSecrets

Scott Bessent grew up in Conway, South Carolina, the oldest of three children. His father was a real estate agent. He attended Yale University, graduating in 1984 with a degree in political science — he had originally planned to pursue journalism but interned with investor Jim Rogers and switched to finance.

Career at Soros Fund Management: In 1991 Bessent joined Soros Fund Management — the investment vehicle of George Soros, who is the largest Democratic donor in American history. He managed the London office from 1991 to 2000. He was part of the team that famously shorted the British pound in September 1992, netting $1 billion in a single day — one of the most famous trades in financial history. He returned to Soros Fund Management as Chief Investment Officer from 2011 to 2015, managing billions in assets.

Key Square Group: In 2015 Bessent founded Key Square Group, his own global macro hedge fund, with a $2 billion anchor investment from Soros himself. The fund focused on geopolitical and economic shifts. His estimated net worth is approximately $600 million.

Political transformation: Bessent's political history is one of the more dramatic transformations in the Cabinet. In 2000 he hosted a fundraiser for Al Gore. He donated to Barack Obama's 2008 campaign and supported Hillary Clinton's 2016 bid. After leaving Soros Fund Management in 2015, his giving shifted Republican. He donated $1 million to Trump's 2017 inaugural committee. By 2024 he had become one of Trump's most important fundraisers — hosting a $57 million fundraiser in Palm Beach — and donated $1.6 million directly to MAGA Inc. and the campaign. He was appointed Treasury Secretary.

As Treasury Secretary: Confirmed 68–29 with significant bipartisan support. He immediately granted DOGE access to the Treasury's payment systems. He was named acting CFPB director and ordered it to halt all work. He has been tasked with implementing a U.S. sovereign wealth fund. He played a key moderating role on tariffs — calling 145% China tariffs "unsustainable" and helping secure a 90-day pause in August 2025. He has clashed with fellow advisor Peter Navarro on tariff strategy. In September 2025 he reportedly threatened to punch a federal housing official at a private dinner. He was also briefly named acting IRS Commissioner in August 2025.
