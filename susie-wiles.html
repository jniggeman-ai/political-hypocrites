Background & RecordSource: Wikipedia · OpenSecrets · Britannica · Vanity Fair

Susie Wiles was born Susan Summerall in New Jersey in 1957 — the daughter of Pat Summerall, the legendary NFL kicker who became one of the most recognizable sports broadcasters in American television history. She graduated from the University of Maryland in 1978 with a degree in English.

Early career: She began working for Rep. Jack Kemp while still in college, then joined Ronald Reagan's 1980 presidential campaign in its scheduling office, followed by the White House Office of Scheduling and then the Labor Department. She worked on Bush-Quayle in 1988. She served as Jacksonville Mayor John Delaney's chief of staff from 1997 to 2000.

Campaign work: She managed Rick Scott's successful 2010 Florida gubernatorial campaign. She ran Ron DeSantis's successful 2018 campaign. She worked on Trump's 2016 and 2020 campaigns — though she was fired from Trump's 2020 campaign by DeSantis, who pressured Trump to let her go. She was later brought back.

Lobbying (2011–2024): Wiles worked as a lobbyist for Ballard Partners from 2011 to 2022, then Mercury Public Affairs from 2022 to 2024. She represented 42 clients including the People's Democratic Party of Nigeria and Venezuelan media company Globovision — a pro-Maduro network. She earned Ballard and Mercury a combined $5.6 million in lobbying fees. Both firms gained significant new business immediately after she was named Chief of Staff.

2024 Campaign: She and Chris LaCivita co-managed Trump's 2024 presidential campaign, which Trump has credited as the most professionally run of his three campaigns. She was widely described as the architect of his victory. Trump named her Chief of Staff two days after winning the election.

Chief of Staff: She is the first woman to serve as White House Chief of Staff. She negotiated a prisoner exchange with Russia in February 2025. She is named in an AP lawsuit after the White House restricted the news agency's access over the Gulf of Mexico naming controversy. She was included in a congressional letter about the White House ballroom — a $300M+ construction project funded by corporate donors including Google, Amazon, and Meta — whose companies have significant regulatory interests before the administration she runs. In December 2025, Vanity Fair published candid interviews in which she described Trump as having "an alcoholic's personality." In March 2026, Trump announced she had been diagnosed with breast cancer and is undergoing treatment.

Daughters: Both of Wiles's daughters work as lobbyists. Katie Wiles was promoted to lead her firm's Washington office the day after her mother was named Chief of Staff. Caroline Wiles, who resigned from a first-term White House role after failing an FBI background check, was promoted to VP of federal affairs at another lobbying firm in January 2025.
