Background

Early Life & Education

Thomas Miller McClintock II was born on July 10, 1956 in Bronxville, Westchester County, New York. His family moved to Thousand Oaks, California in 1965. He graduated from UCLA in 1978 with a degree in political science. Before entering electoral politics, he worked as a journalist and public policy analyst.

California Legislative Career (1982–2008)

McClintock was first elected to the California State Assembly in 1982 and served until 1992. After an unsuccessful 1992 congressional bid, he returned to the Assembly in 1996. In 2000 he was elected to the California State Senate, where he served until 2008 — giving him a total of 22 years in the California legislature, during which he became one of its most recognized conservative voices on fiscal policy.

He ran for California State Controller twice — losing by 2 points in 1994 and by just 0.2 percentage points (16,811 votes) in 2002. He ran for Governor in the 2003 recall election, finishing third with 14% behind Arnold Schwarzenegger and Cruz Bustamante. He ran for Lieutenant Governor in 2006, losing to Democrat John Garamendi 49%–45%.

Congressional Career

McClintock was elected to Congress in 2008 and has represented the Sierra Nevada foothills district — Lake Tahoe, the Gold Country, Yosemite, and Kings Canyon — ever since. He is among the most consistently conservative fiscal votes in the House. The National Taxpayers Union has rated him the best vote for taxpayers in the House four times, most recently in 2020.

He chairs the House Judiciary Subcommittee on Immigration Integrity, Security, and Enforcement, and serves on the Natural Resources Committee. He recently authored the Shut Down Sanctuary Policies Act, which passed the House Judiciary Committee in March 2026.

Key Votes

Election Integrity · January 7, 2021
Voted to Certify the 2020 Election — Against His Party
After the January 6 Capitol attack, McClintock broke with the majority of House Republicans and voted to certify the 2020 presidential election results, accepting both Arizona and Pennsylvania's electoral votes for Joe Biden. He was one of a minority of House Republicans to do so. He tweeted: "Haven't we done enough to divide the country and abuse the Constitution?" Note: GovTrack separately flagged that McClintock joined a Supreme Court filing before January 6 that sought to discard electoral votes in four swing states — a position he ultimately did not vote to sustain on the floor.
Fiscal Policy · Career
Rated "Best Vote for Taxpayers" Four Times by National Taxpayers Union
The National Taxpayers Union has rated McClintock the House member with the best voting record for taxpayers four separate times, most recently in 2020. Citizens Against Government Waste has also designated him a "perfect vote" for fighting wasteful spending.
Immigration · February 2026
Authored Shut Down Sanctuary Policies Act
McClintock introduced H.R. 7640, the Shut Down Sanctuary Policies Act, which passed the House Judiciary Committee in March 2026 and was placed on the Union Calendar. The bill would restrict local governments from implementing sanctuary policies that limit cooperation with federal immigration enforcement.
Environment
Opposes Federal Wildfire Management Restrictions
McClintock has consistently argued that federal restrictions on forest management — including limits on logging and controlled burns — contribute to catastrophic wildfires. He authored the Proven Forest Management Act to expand categorical exclusions for forest management in national forests. He has cited data that California's 2020 wildfires produced more CO2 than the state prevented through all emissions restrictions since 2003.

War & Foreign Policy

General Posture
Fiscal Conservative — Skeptical of Overseas Commitments
As a fiscal conservative, McClintock has at times questioned the cost of open-ended overseas military commitments and foreign aid. He is a member of the Republican Study Committee, which has generally supported defense spending but pushed for constraints on foreign aid.
Immigration Enforcement
Chairs Subcommittee on Immigration Integrity, Security, and Enforcement
McClintock's primary policy focus in the 119th Congress has been immigration enforcement through his subcommittee chairmanship. He has used the role to advance legislation restricting sanctuary policies and expanding cooperation between local law enforcement and federal immigration authorities.
