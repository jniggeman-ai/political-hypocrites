Background & RecordSource: Wikipedia · DOT · ProPublica · OpenSecrets

Sean Duffy grew up in Hayward, Wisconsin as the tenth of twelve children. He became a professional lumberjack, competing in the Lumberjack World Championships — winning multiple titles in speed climbing. In 1997 he appeared on MTV's The Real World: Boston, where he met his future wife Rachel Campos. He later appeared on Road Rules: All Stars.

He earned a marketing degree from St. Mary's College of Minnesota and a law degree from William Mitchell College of Law in 1999. He served as District Attorney of Ashland County, Wisconsin from 2002 to 2010.

Congress (2011–2019): Duffy represented Wisconsin's 7th District for nearly a decade, serving on the House Financial Services Committee. He resigned in 2019 to prioritize his family — his wife was pregnant with their ninth child, who was born with a heart condition. After leaving Congress, he joined lobbying firm BGR Group as senior counsel representing clients including Enterprise Products and aviation industry interests. He then co-hosted Fox Business's The Bottom Line starting in 2023.

Secretary of Transportation: Confirmed 77–22 in one of the more bipartisan Cabinet votes. He also briefly served as acting NASA administrator from July to December 2025. As DOT Secretary he has: reversed fuel economy standards set by Pete Buttigieg; cut California High-Speed Rail funding; cut Texas Central Railway funding; threatened to withhold $14 billion in federal transit funding from New York's MTA over subway crime; froze the $16 billion Hudson Tunnel Project over DEI concerns; eliminated $54 million in university research grants he called "woke and wasteful"; and worked to increase air traffic controllers after widespread FAA staffing concerns.

Son-in-law ethics controversy: A March 2026 ProPublica investigation found that transportation industry lobbyists and executives — including a CEO whose company is seeking a DOT-issued port license — donated tens of thousands to the congressional campaign of Duffy's son-in-law Michael Alfonso, who had never run for office and has no political experience. Duffy transferred $1 million from his old campaign PAC to a super PAC supporting Alfonso. Legal experts noted the arrangement creates a clear appearance of conflict even if technically legal.
