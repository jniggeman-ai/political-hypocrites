Background

Early Life & Military Service

Charles Michael Thompson was born on January 24, 1951 in St. Helena, California — the heart of the Napa Valley wine region. He dropped out of high school to enlist in the U.S. Army during the Vietnam War. He served as a staff sergeant and platoon leader with the 173rd Airborne Brigade, was wounded in combat, and was awarded the Purple Heart. He later served as an instructor at the Army's Airborne School.

After returning home, Thompson worked as the maintenance supervisor at Beringer Winery in Napa Valley. Using the GI Bill, he returned to school — attending Napa Valley Community College and graduating from California State University, Chico. He subsequently earned a Master's degree in Public Administration from CSU Chico. He became the first in his family to earn a college degree. He later taught Public Administration and State Government at San Francisco State University and CSU Chico.

Political Career

Thompson was elected to the California State Senate in 1990, representing the 2nd District. He was the first Vietnam veteran elected to the California State Senate. He chaired the powerful Budget Committee during his time in the State Senate.

He was elected to Congress in 1998, first taking office in January 1999. His district — variously numbered over redistricting cycles but currently CA-4 — covers Napa Valley and surrounding wine country, including all or parts of Lake, Napa, Solano, Sonoma, and Yolo counties.

Thompson is a member of the Blue Dog Coalition, the caucus of moderate Democrats committed to fiscal responsibility and bipartisan problem-solving. He is also co-founder and co-chair of the bipartisan Congressional Wine Caucus (over 215 members) and has twice co-chaired the Congressional Sportsmen's Caucus. He is a small vineyard owner in Napa Valley.

He is running for re-election in 2026 and faces an intra-party primary challenge from Eric Jones, in the context of a Politico-reported Democratic effort to unseat senior incumbents.

Key Votes

Gun Policy · Ongoing
Co-Chair, House Gun Violence Prevention Task Force
Thompson was appointed Chair of the House Gun Violence Prevention Task Force following the Sandy Hook school shooting in December 2012. He is a gun owner and hunter who identifies as a Second Amendment supporter, while simultaneously advocating for expanded background checks on all firearm sales.
Legislation · 2026
Introduced Tariff Refund Credit Bill
In February 2026, Thompson introduced H.R. 7636, which would amend the Internal Revenue Code to create an individual tariff refund credit — providing direct relief to consumers from tariff costs.
Legislation · 2026
Prevent Presidential Profiteering Act
In February 2026, Thompson introduced H.R. 7381, the Prevent Presidential Profiteering Act, with 15 co-sponsors. The bill would restrict financial activities by sitting presidents that could constitute self-dealing.
Environment · Mixed Record
Received Mixed Ratings from Environmental Groups
Despite representing a Northern California district and receiving a Sierra Club Edgar Wayburn Award, Thompson's environmental record has included votes that drew criticism from some environmental organizations: he voted for the Healthy Forests Initiative under George W. Bush, against limits on new commercial logging roads in Alaska's Tongass National Forest, and against limits on bear baiting. The American Wilderness Coalition gave him a B rating in 2003 and an A+ in 2004.
Attendance
1.3% Missed Vote Rate (Career)
From January 1999 through March 2026, Thompson missed 224 of 17,172 roll call votes — a career missed vote rate of 1.3%, better than the median of 2.1% among currently serving members.

War & Foreign Policy

Iraq War · 2002
Pre-War Fact-Finding Trip to Iraq; Skeptic of WMD Claims
In late 2002, Thompson joined Representatives Jim McDermott and David Bonior on a trip to Iraq. During the visit, they expressed skepticism about the Bush administration's claims that Saddam Hussein was stockpiling weapons of mass destruction. The trip was later scrutinized when a Michigan charity official was charged with helping underwrite the trip on behalf of Iraqi intelligence — though Thompson's office had already organized the trip independently before the charity offered to pay expenses.
Military Service
Vietnam Veteran — Purple Heart Recipient
Thompson served as a staff sergeant and platoon leader with the Army's 173rd Airborne Brigade in Vietnam, where he was wounded in action and received the Purple Heart. He has co-chaired the Military Veterans Caucus in Congress and led legislation on veteran PTSD treatment, TBI care, and GI Bill modernization.
