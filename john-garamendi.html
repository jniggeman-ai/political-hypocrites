Background

Early Life & Education

John Garamendi was born at Camp Blanding military base in Florida and raised on his family's cattle ranch in Mokelumne Hill, California. He earned a B.A. in business from UC Berkeley in 1966, where he was a Wheeler Scholar, a second-team All-American offensive guard in football, two-time All-Pacific Coast Conference, and the 1964 West Coast heavyweight wrestling champion. He then earned an MBA from Harvard Business School. He and his wife Patti served in the Peace Corps in Ethiopia from 1966 to 1968, teaching 7th and 8th graders. He continues to raise Angus cattle and grow pears on the family ranch.

California Political Career (1974–2009)

Garamendi was elected to the California State Assembly in 1974 at age 27, flipping a Republican seat and helping deliver a Democratic supermajority. He served one term before moving to the State Senate, where he served four terms (1976–1990) and became Senate Majority Leader. He authored the first solar, wind, and energy conservation tax credits in California, the Rural Health Act, and California's first assault weapons ban.

He ran for governor four times (1982, 1994, 2003, and planned to run in 2010 before pivoting to Congress). He served as California's first elected Insurance Commissioner from 1991–1995 and again from 2003–2007, earning a reputation as an aggressive consumer advocate. President Clinton appointed him Deputy Secretary of the Interior in 1995, where he led environmental restoration efforts in the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta. He was elected Lieutenant Governor in 2006.

Congressional Career

Garamendi won a November 2009 special election to fill the seat vacated by Rep. Ellen Tauscher and has served since. Now in his eighth term, he represents CA-8, covering parts of Contra Costa and Solano Counties. He serves on the Armed Services Committee (Subcommittees on Readiness and Strategic Forces) and the Transportation and Infrastructure Committee. He is a member of the Congressional Progressive Caucus.

Notable Controversies

Insurance Scandal · 1991–2005
Executive Life Insurance — Sale to French Front for Government-Owned Bank
As California Insurance Commissioner in 1991, Garamendi seized Executive Life — then California's largest life insurer, which had become insolvent after overloading on junk bonds — and sold its assets in a competitive bid process to a consortium of French companies. A whistleblower later revealed that the winning consortium was a front for Credit Lyonnais, a bank owned by the French government. California law prohibited government-owned foreign entities from owning California insurers. The fraud was concealed from Garamendi during the bidding process. The scandal became a multi-decade legal case in both the U.S. and France, with criminal charges filed. Policyholders, who suffered losses described by one advocacy group leader as "perhaps $5 billion," blamed Garamendi for the outcome, though the state's own lawsuit asserted the conspiracy had been concealed from him. A 2005 settlement recovered $516.5 million from Credit Lyonnais and related parties. Final settlement with remaining defendant Artemis continued for years.

War & Foreign Policy

Committee Role
Ranking Member, House Armed Services Subcommittee on Readiness
Garamendi serves as the top Democrat on the House Armed Services Subcommittee on Readiness, overseeing military preparedness, training, infrastructure, and health care for servicemembers. He has used the role to push for climate resilience investments at military installations and expanded legal protections for troops harmed by toxic exposure.
Iran · 2026
Voted for War Powers Resolution — Opposed Trump's Iran Strikes
Garamendi voted for the House War Powers Resolution directing the president to remove U.S. forces from unauthorized hostilities in Iran, citing the deaths of seven American servicemembers and the lack of a congressional authorization for the military action.
Jones Act
Issued Statement Against Jones Act Waiver on Gasoline Prices
In 2026, Garamendi publicly opposed the Trump administration's decision to grant a Jones Act waiver to address high gasoline prices, calling it "a misguided attempt" that undermined domestic maritime industry workers.
