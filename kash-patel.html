What FBI Directors Typically Look Like vs. Kash Patel

Previous FBI directors have included federal judges, U.S. attorneys who ran major offices (New York, Chicago), and career prosecutors with decades of senior law enforcement experience. As New Yorker writer Marc Fisher noted: Patel "has no prior law enforcement or intelligence experience" at a senior level — "lack of familiarity with what the FBI does." Trump confirmed appointees including Barr, Haspel, Esper, and Bolton — all Republicans — opposed his nomination. Source: The New Yorker · NPR
Documented ControversiesSource: CNN · Newsweek · NPR · PBS · The New Yorker · Sen. Durbin floor speech · Federal lawsuit
Told Senior Official He Needed to Fire Trump-Case Agents "To Keep His Own Job"Federal Lawsuit

According to a federal lawsuit filed by three fired senior FBI officials, Patel told then-acting FBI Director Brian Driscoll in an August 2025 conversation: "There was nothing he or Driscoll could do to stop these or any other firings because the FBI tried to put the President in jail and he hasn't forgotten it." The lawsuit states Patel acknowledged he needed to fire agents who worked on Trump cases in order to keep his own position — regardless of whether the agents chose those assignments themselves. Source: CNN · federal complaint

60-Name "Deep State" Enemies List in Published BookDocumented

Patel published a book called "Government Gangsters" that includes an explicit enemies list of 60 names — people he labeled "members of the deep state," including distinguished public servants from both political parties. He now leads the FBI — one of the most powerful law enforcement and surveillance agencies in the world — with a published list of people he considers enemies. Sen. Dick Durbin read this list on the Senate floor during Patel's confirmation hearings. Source: Sen. Durbin floor speech

Three Senior FBI Directors Sued Him for Unlawful TerminationActive Litigation

Former Acting FBI Director Brian Driscoll, Assistant Director Steven Jensen, and Las Vegas Field Office Director Spencer Evans filed a 68-page federal lawsuit against Patel alleging their firings were part of a White House-directed political purge. Attorney Mark Zaid: "You talk about weaponization. You are the textbook definition of weaponization." The lawsuit portrays Patel and Deputy Director Dan Bongino as obsessed with social media optics, directing staff to post "FBI wins" and floating the idea of suing conservative influencers who criticized them. Source: CNN · federal complaint

Alleged Perjury at Confirmation HearingAlleged

Sen. Dick Durbin alleged on the Senate floor that Patel likely committed perjury when he said under oath "I don't know what's going on right now" regarding firings at the FBI. Durbin cited multiple whistleblowers who provided evidence that Patel was actively directing firings of career agents while he was still a private citizen awaiting confirmation. Source: Sen. Durbin floor speech · Senate records

Charlie Kirk Assassination MishandlingDocumented

Hours after conservative activist Charlie Kirk was shot and killed in Utah, Patel posted on social media that a suspect was in custody. Local authorities said the individual had only been questioned and released. The actual suspect wasn't in custody for another 33 hours. Conservative commentators including Laura Loomer and Christopher Rufo called for Patel's resignation over his performance. Former FBI agent Asha Rangappa told PBS the criticism was warranted. Source: Newsweek · PBS

Threatened to "Shut Down" FBI Headquarters, Turn It Into "Museum of the Deep State"Documented

In a YouTube interview before his confirmation, Patel stated he would "shut down" FBI headquarters, convert it into a "museum of the deep state," and reassign its 7,000 employees. In a separate 2023 Steve Bannon podcast, he made statements about targeting journalists who truthfully reported on the 2020 election. Source: AA.com.tr · NPR · Leadership Conference

Promoted Fake Vaccine "Detox" Supplements on Truth SocialDocumented

On Trump's Truth Social platform, Patel promoted a dietary supplement marketed as a COVID-19 vaccine "detoxification system," writing: "Spike the Vax, order this homerun kit to rid your body of the harms of the vax." No evidence supports the existence of such a detox. The FDA has not approved any such product. Source: AA.com.tr profile

Opposed by Trump's Own Former OfficialsDocumented

Former Trump AG William Barr, CIA Director Gina Haspel, Defense Secretary Mark Esper, and National Security Advisor John Bolton — all Trump appointees — opposed Patel's nomination as FBI Director. Sen. Durbin: "Consider who is warning us about Mr. Patel: former Trump officials who know him." Source: Sen. Durbin floor speech

Kash Patel & The Epstein FilesSource: NPR Fresh Air interview with Marc Fisher · Newsweek

This is one of the most significant and well-documented reversals of Kash Patel's career.

Before becoming FBI Director: Patel regularly amplified conspiracy theories about Epstein's death on his and others' podcasts. He demanded the "client list" be released and accused the government of protecting "the world's foremost predators" by refusing to release the names.

After becoming FBI Director and reading the files: Patel approved a memo — released quietly after the July 4th holiday weekend — concluding that "no further disclosure would be appropriate or warranted." He now says Epstein's death was "absolutely a suicide," there is "no client list," "no evidence to the contrary," and that Trump's presence in the Epstein files is "minimal and benign."

NPR's Marc Fisher: "This almost complete reversal on Patel's part is bizarrely accompanied now by Kash Patel saying, how can you not believe me?"

Patel is not personally mentioned in the released Epstein court documents. However his handling of the Epstein file investigation — and his dramatic reversal on releasing information — is one of the most scrutinized aspects of his tenure. Source: NPR · Newsweek
