BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · Encyclopedia of Alabama · Alabama Living · GovTrack

Michael Dennis Rogers was born July 16, 1958 in Hammond, Indiana. His family left Calhoun County in 1957 in search of work and returned in 1967. His father was a firefighter at Anniston Army Depot and his mother was a textile worker. He is a sixth-generation East Alabamian. He graduated from Saks High School in Anniston, earned a BA in Political Science and an MPA from Jacksonville State University, and received his JD from Birmingham School of Law, graduating with honors. He and his wife Beth both attended law school at night while working. He opened a law practice in Anniston and was a small business owner.

Early political career: At age 28 Rogers was elected to the Calhoun County Commission — the youngest commissioner ever and the first Republican. He was elected to the Alabama House of Representatives in 1994 and became minority leader in his second term. In 2002 he won Alabama's 3rd Congressional District seat, defeating Democrat Joe Turnham Jr. 50%–48%. He has held the seat since.

House Armed Services Committee: Rogers is Chair of the House Armed Services Committee — the first Alabamian ever to hold that position. The committee authorizes approximately half of all federal discretionary spending. He has used the role to advocate for Anniston Army Depot, Fort Benning, and the relocation of Space Operations Command from Colorado to Redstone Arsenal in Huntsville. In September 2025, President Trump announced Space Command's permanent headquarters would move to Huntsville.

U.S. Space Force: Rogers was a key initiator of the creation of the U.S. Space Force as a separate branch of the armed forces in 2019. He has described space as a "warfighting domain" and has advocated for building U.S. space capabilities to counter China and Russia.

Speaker vote altercation (January 2023): During the 14th ballot of the House Speaker election, Rogers lunged at Rep. Matt Gaetz after Gaetz spoke with Kevin McCarthy. Rep. Richard Hudson physically restrained Rogers, pulling him back by the shoulder and then the face. The Associated Press reported Rogers "charged" at Gaetz. The incident occurred as Rogers was in line to become chair of the Armed Services Committee — a position Gaetz was reportedly seeking a subcommittee chairmanship on.

SecDef consideration: Following Trump's 2024 re-election, Rogers was reportedly contacted by Trump's transition team as a potential nominee for Secretary of Defense. He was not ultimately nominated.
