BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · Encyclopedia of Alabama · Ballotpedia · GovTrack

Dale Whitney Strong was born May 8, 1970 in Monrovia, Alabama. His father, Horace N. Strong, was a founding member of the Monrovia Fire-Rescue Department. Strong began volunteering as a firefighter at age 13 and remained an active volunteer firefighter throughout his time as a county commissioner. He graduated from Sparkman High School in 1988, earned a BS in Business Administration from Athens State University, and an EMT license from the University of Alabama in Huntsville. Before politics he worked in public relations for First Alabama Bank and as a sales representative for Solvay S.A.

Madison County Commission (1996–2023): Strong was elected to the Madison County Commission in 1996 — the youngest elected Republican official in Alabama at the time. He was elected Commission Chairman in 2012 and served until 2023. During his tenure he focused on economic development, road improvements, school construction, and recruited federal agencies to Redstone Arsenal including NASA and the FBI. Yellowhammer News called him one of the three most influential people in North Alabama in 2018.

Confederate monument controversy: As county chair, Strong was involved in the relocation of a Confederate monument. Members of the Morgan County Republican Party and Heritage Protection of North Alabama attempted to prevent Strong's name from appearing on primary ballots as a result. He won the primary and then the general election by a two-to-one margin. Source: Encyclopedia of Alabama

Congress (2023–present): Strong succeeded Mo Brooks in representing Alabama's 5th District, which includes Huntsville, Redstone Arsenal, and Marshall Space Flight Center. He ran unopposed in 2024. In the 119th Congress he serves on the House Appropriations Committee and chairs the Homeland Security Subcommittee on Emergency Management and Technology.

Key legislation introduced: Finish It Act (requiring DOD to transfer border construction materials to states); Deport Illegal Voters Act; No More Funding for NPR Act; anti-China land ownership bills; Protecting America's Agricultural Land from Foreign Harm Act; SHIELD Against CCP Act; Promoting American Patriotism in Our Schools Act (mandating Pledge of Allegiance and flag display in schools receiving federal funding). None of the major bills became law as of March 2026. Source: Encyclopedia of Alabama · GovTrack

War & Foreign Policy RecordSource: Encyclopedia of Alabama · Wikipedia · GovTrack

Ukraine (2024) — Voted Against Aid: Strong voted against the Ukraine Security Supplemental Appropriations Act, opposing continued military, economic, and humanitarian aid to Ukraine during the Russian invasion. The Encyclopedia of Alabama notes that some of the weapons covered by the bill are manufactured in his district. He voted against aid alongside fellow Alabama Rep. Barry Moore.

Israel (2024) — Voted For Aid: Voted for the Israel Security Supplemental, providing continued military support to Israel during the Gaza War.

China — Anti-Influence Legislation: Strong has introduced multiple bills targeting Chinese influence in the U.S., including the Protecting America's Agricultural Land from Foreign Harm Act (barring Chinese-linked individuals from buying U.S. agricultural land) and the SHIELD Against CCP Act (creating a DHS unit to counter Chinese security threats). Source: Encyclopedia of Alabama

Space Command Relocation: Strong has actively lobbied alongside other Alabama delegation members to relocate Space Operations Command from Colorado Springs to Redstone Arsenal in Huntsville. President Trump announced the move in September 2025. Source: Encyclopedia of Alabama
