Background & RecordSource: OpenSecrets · Wikipedia · DOI · Britannica

Doug Burgum grew up on a grain farm in Arthur, North Dakota — population roughly 400. His father, a World War II veteran, died from a brain tumor when Burgum was a high school freshman. He attended North Dakota State University, then earned an MBA from Stanford in 1980, where he befriended future Microsoft CEO Steve Ballmer.

In 1983 he mortgaged the farmland his father left him to invest $250,000 in Great Plains Software, a small Fargo company building accounting software. He became CEO, took the company public in 1997, and sold it to Microsoft for $1.1 billion in stock in 2001 — one of the largest tech exits in North Dakota history. He then worked at Microsoft as a senior vice president for six years.

In 2007 he co-founded the Kilbourne Group (real estate development) and Arthur Ventures (private equity). He joined the board of Australian software company Atlassian and chaired SuccessFactors, which was acquired for $3.4 billion in 2012.

Governor of North Dakota (2016–2024): Burgum ran for governor as a political outsider with no prior elected experience, riding the same anti-establishment wave that elected Trump. He won the 2016 primary in a massive upset and took nearly 77% of the general election vote. He was reelected easily in 2020. As governor of the third-largest oil-producing state, he presided over record oil production and enacted major income tax cuts. He signed a near-total abortion ban and legislation targeting transgender rights. He pledged to make North Dakota carbon neutral by 2030 through carbon capture — not renewables.

Presidential run (2023): Burgum briefly ran for the 2024 Republican presidential nomination, emphasizing his business background. His campaign failed to gain traction and he dropped out in December 2023, endorsing Trump. He emerged as a serious VP contender before Trump selected JD Vance.

Secretary of the Interior: Confirmed 79–18 in one of the most bipartisan votes of the Cabinet class. His first act was sending a department-wide email calling for reduced regulation and expanded natural resource extraction. He signed orders rolling back Inflation Reduction Act regulations, opened 1.53 million acres of the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge to oil drilling, cracked down on wind power, and promoted coal. Trump also made him chairman of the new National Energy Dominance Council — giving him sweeping authority to coordinate energy policy across all federal agencies.

The Department of the Interior manages roughly one-fifth of all U.S. land and waters — including all national parks, wildlife refuges, and Bureau of Land Management land. It also oversees relations with federally recognized Native American tribes.

Financial DisclosuresSource: OGE · Senate Financial Disclosures

Net Worth: Burgum's wealth stems primarily from the $1.1 billion Microsoft stock he received when selling Great Plains Software in 2001. As a former tech CEO and venture capitalist, he holds a range of investments.

Conflicts of Interest: As Interior Secretary and chairman of the National Energy Dominance Council, Burgum oversees federal lands and energy policy that directly affect the oil, gas, and mining industries he has long been affiliated with. He participated in a Mar-a-Lago "energy roundtable" in May 2024 — alongside ExxonMobil and API executives — where Trump asked attendees for $1 billion in campaign donations and promised to roll back environmental regulations. Burgum was present.

Arthur Ventures / Kilbourne Group: His private equity and real estate holdings may intersect with Interior Department decisions on land use, permitting, and development.
