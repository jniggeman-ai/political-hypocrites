Biography

Markwayne Mullin was born on July 26, 1977, in Tulsa, Oklahoma — the youngest of seven children. His first name is the result of his parents being unable to decide between naming him Mark or Wayne after two of his uncles; his mother put both names on the birth certificate and never changed it. He grew up on a ranch in Westville, Oklahoma, near the Arkansas border, and is an enrolled member of the Cherokee Nation — the second Cherokee citizen ever to serve in the U.S. Senate and the first in nearly two decades.

As a child, Mullin had a clubfoot requiring leg braces and multiple surgeries, as well as a speech impediment. He attended Stilwell High School where he wrestled, then attended Missouri Valley College on a wrestling scholarship. When his father's health deteriorated in 1998, he left college to take over the family plumbing business. He built Mullin Plumbing from a six-person company into the largest service company in Oklahoma, eventually expanding into Mullin Environmental, Mullin Services, Mullin Properties, and Rowan's Steakhouse. He sold the plumbing business to a private equity firm in December 2021 for an undisclosed sum. In his late twenties, he briefly pursued a professional MMA career, going 5-0 as an undefeated welterweight.

He was elected to the U.S. House of Representatives in 2012, representing Oklahoma's 2nd congressional district — one of the poorest districts in the country. He served five terms before running for Senate in the 2022 special election to fill the seat vacated by retiring Senator Jim Inhofe. He won. He has been a Trump loyalist in the Senate, later becoming a key liaison between Trump and the Republican Senate caucus.

Trump nominated him to replace Kristi Noem as DHS Secretary on March 5, 2026 — the same day Noem was fired. The Senate Homeland Security Committee advanced his nomination 8–7 on March 19, 2026, with committee chairman Rand Paul being the sole Republican to vote against him. Democrat John Fetterman crossed the aisle to vote yes. A full Senate confirmation vote is expected in late March 2026.

Confirmation Hearing — March 18, 2026
The Rand Paul confrontation. Committee chairman Rand Paul — a Republican — was the only Republican to vote against advancing Mullin's nomination. Paul cited Mullin's comment calling him a "freaking snake" and saying he "understood completely" why Paul's neighbor had tackled and assaulted him in 2017, leaving Paul with broken ribs and requiring multiple surgeries. "I just wonder if someone who applauds violence against their political opponents is the right person to lead an agency that has struggled to accept limits to the proper use of force?" Paul said. Mullin shot back, calling Paul's questioning a "character assassination" and saying: "For you to say I'm a liar, sir, that's not accurate." After the hearing Paul told reporters he would not vote for Mullin's confirmation.
The "classified" trip. During the hearing, senators pressed Mullin about a foreign trip he took while a House member that he repeatedly described as "classified" — declining to provide details publicly. Some committee members relocated to a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility to hear more from Mullin privately. Democrats said his handling of questions about the trip raised transparency concerns. "Throughout the nomination process, he has failed to be forthright and transparent," said ranking member Gary Peters (D-MI).
Minneapolis shootings. Senators pressed Mullin about the January 2026 killing of U.S. citizens Renée Good and Alex Pretti by federal agents in Minneapolis — the controversy that ultimately sank Noem. Mullin stopped short of apologizing for comments he made in the immediate aftermath of the shootings and declined to fully distance himself from Noem's handling. He promised a different leadership style focused on "empowering people."
Fetterman crosses aisle. Democratic Senator John Fetterman of Pennsylvania voted to advance Mullin's nomination — a rare bipartisan moment. Fetterman posted on X: "In January, I called on the president to fire Noem — and he did. I truly approached the confirmation of my colleague and friend, Senator Mullin, with an open-mind. We must reopen DHS. My AYE is rooted in a strong committed, constructive working relationship with Senator Mullin for our nation's security."
Committee Vote Breakdown

The 8–7 vote to advance Mullin's nomination included one Republican breaking with the party (Rand Paul — NO) and one Democrat crossing over (John Fetterman — YES). Democrats voting NO included Gary Peters (MI), Maggie Hassan (NH), Richard Blumenthal (CT), Andy Kim (NJ), Ruben Gallego (AZ), and Elissa Slotkin (MI). The full Senate procedural vote advanced 54–37 with two Democrats supporting.

Controversies & Ethics Concerns
Ethics
Ran ads identifying himself as owner of Mullin Plumbing for months after selling the company to private equity.
Mullin sold Mullin Plumbing to CenterOak Partners in December 2021. For several months in 2022 he continued airing political ads in which he identified himself as the business's owner. When confronted, Mullin said he was still "involved" with the company. Critics said this was misleading to voters who were electing him partly on the basis of his identity as a small business owner.
Ethics
Received approximately $370,000 in federal stimulus funds through Cherokee and Muscogee Nations while publicly opposing federal stimulus spending.
Mullin has been a consistent critic of federal spending and stimulus. Reports revealed Mullin Plumbing had received approximately $370,000 in federal stimulus funds. Mullin said he was unaware of the source of the funds. Records obtained by Tulsa World showed the firm was aware. Mullin denied personal knowledge.
Conduct
Challenged Teamsters union president Sean O'Brien to a fistfight during a Senate committee hearing in 2023.
During a 2023 Senate HELP Committee hearing, Mullin told O'Brien: "Stand your butt up." O'Brien mocked him. Committee chairman Bernie Sanders ordered Mullin to calm down. The exchange went viral. Mullin later told Fox News that violence on the Senate floor, like in the 1800s, should perhaps "come back." During his DHS confirmation hearing, Rand Paul cited Mullin's conduct and rhetoric as disqualifying for leading an agency managing the use of force.
Term Limits
Broke his own pledge to serve no more than three terms in the House.
Mullin campaigned on a pledge to serve no more than three terms in the House. After three terms, he and his wife "prayed about it" and decided to run again. He won a fourth and fifth term before running for the Senate. Voters in his district expressed frustration but it did not cost him the election.
Classified Activity
Vague about a foreign "classified" trip taken while a House member.
During his DHS confirmation hearing, Mullin described a foreign trip: "In 2015, I was asked to train with a very small contingency and go to a certain area." He declined to elaborate publicly. Some committee members moved to a secure facility to hear more. Democrats flagged this as a transparency concern for someone being nominated to lead one of the largest national security agencies in the government.
Legislative Record — Key Positions
January 6, 2021: On January 6, Mullin helped Capitol Police physically block the doors to the House chamber during the riot. However, he had earlier echoed false claims that the 2020 election was stolen and said he planned to contest the electoral results. He said: "When there's as many questions still out there on the electoral votes and the votes that took place, we had to challenge it. That's what Congress is supposed to do on January 6."
Healthcare: Voted repeatedly to repeal the Affordable Care Act. Analysts warned that his support for the "Big Beautiful Bill" could strip healthcare from 110,000 Oklahomans while providing tax breaks for the top 1%.
Immigration: Strong supporter of Trump's immigration enforcement agenda. Now nominated to lead the agency responsible for implementing it. His confirmation hearing raised questions about his specific immigration policy positions given his limited background in immigration law or enforcement.
FEMA / Oklahoma disasters: Mullin has worked with FEMA on Oklahoma disaster relief — one of his few areas of DHS-relevant experience. Oklahoma regularly faces severe weather events including tornadoes. This is the primary area where senators identified genuine DHS-related experience.
Experience gap: Multiple senators and analysts noted Mullin has no senior law enforcement experience, no immigration enforcement background, and no national security experience in the traditional sense — mirroring concerns raised about Noem. His primary qualifications cited by supporters are his loyalty to Trump and his reputation as a no-nonsense direct communicator.
