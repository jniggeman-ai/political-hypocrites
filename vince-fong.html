Background

Early Life & Heritage

Vince Fong was born on October 24, 1979 in Bakersfield, California, to Chinese immigrant parents. His father came from Hong Kong and worked in a Bakersfield pharmacy. As a child, Fong struggled with a stutter — an experience he has cited as formative in developing his communication skills and community focus. He attended West High School in Bakersfield, earned a B.A. from UCLA, and a Master's degree from Princeton University. He is a lifetime member of the National Rifle Association.

Pre-Congressional Career

Fong began his career as an aide to Congressman Bill Thomas, then Chair of the House Ways and Means Committee, working on international trade policy to expand market access for American farmers and small businesses. Through that work he met Thomas's district director — Kevin McCarthy. Fong then returned to Kern County and served as McCarthy's district director for nearly a decade, managing public policy issues for the Central Valley region. He was also elected to the Kern County Republican Central Committee and served on the boards of Goodwill Industries of South Central California and Honor Flight Kern County, which sends WWII, Korean War, and Vietnam veterans to Washington to visit their memorials.

Path to Congress — Dual Candidacy Controversy

When Kevin McCarthy resigned from Congress effective December 31, 2023, a special election was called. Fong initially declined to run, then reversed course and announced his candidacy — despite already having filed for re-election to the California State Assembly. California Secretary of State Shirley Weber ruled Fong ineligible due to a state law prohibiting candidates from running for two offices simultaneously. Fong sued; a Superior Court judge ruled in his favor. Weber appealed, and the California Legislature moved to pass a bill blocking dual candidacies. Fong ultimately prevailed legally and won the special election runoff on May 21, 2024, to succeed McCarthy. He was subsequently re-elected to a full term in November 2024.

Key Votes

Environment · Bipartisan
Save Our Sequoias Act — Passed House Unanimously
Fong's H.R. 2709, the Save Our Sequoias (SOS) Act, passed the full House unanimously. The legislation addresses the existential threat to giant sequoia trees from wildfire, drought, and bark beetles, directing federal agencies to take emergency action to protect and restore sequoia groves in the Sierra Nevada.
Energy
Opposed California CARB Cap-and-Invest Proposal
Fong urgently called on Governor Newsom, CARB Chair Lauren Sanchez, and the California Energy Commission to reconsider CARB's Cap-and-Invest carbon pricing proposal, arguing it would impose undue costs on Central Valley residents and businesses.
Election · 2024
Won McCarthy's Seat Despite Dual Candidacy Ruling Battle
Fong's path to Congress required defeating a ruling by the California Secretary of State that he was ineligible to run due to his simultaneous Assembly filing. A court sided with him, and he won the May 2024 special election runoff to succeed McCarthy.
