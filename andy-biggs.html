BackgroundSource: Wikipedia · GovTrack · Ballotpedia

Andrew Steven Biggs was born November 7, 1958 in Tucson, Arizona. He earned a BA from the University of Utah, a JD from Arizona State University, and an MA in Government from Harvard University. He is an Arizona native who has spent his career in Gilbert and the East Valley. He served in the Arizona House of Representatives from 2003 to 2011 and the Arizona Senate from 2011 to 2017, serving as Senate President from 2013 to 2017.

2016 primary — 27 votes: Biggs ran in the 2016 Republican primary for Arizona's 5th Congressional District to replace retiring Rep. Matt Salmon. He led the four-way primary field by just nine votes over businesswoman Christine Jones on election night. A recanvass boosted his margin to 16 votes and an automatic recount confirmed him as the nominee by 27 votes out of over 100,000 cast. He won the general election 64%–36%. Source: Wikipedia

Freedom Caucus chair: Biggs served as chairman of the House Freedom Caucus from 2019 to 2022, making him one of the most influential far-right voices in Congress during that period. He ran twice for Speaker of the House — losing to Kevin McCarthy in a 2022 conference vote 188–31, and receiving 10 votes on the first ballot in the 2023 Speaker election before withdrawing. Source: Wikipedia

Governor's race (2026): In April 2025, President Trump co-endorsed Biggs and Karrin Taylor Robson for Arizona governor — the same race. David Schweikert entered the primary in September 2025. Biggs announced he would not seek re-election to the House. His district was previously represented by Senators John McCain and Jeff Flake. Source: Wikipedia

Personal loss (2025): Biggs's daughter Cosette died of cancer in 2025 at age 37, leaving behind a husband and three children. Source: Wikipedia
