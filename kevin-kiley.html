Background

Early Life & Education

Kevin Kiley was born on January 30, 1985. He grew up in the Granite Bay area of Placer County. He graduated from Harvard University with a B.A., then earned a J.D. from Yale Law School, and later a Master's in Education from Loyola Marymount University.

Pre-Congressional Career

After law school, Kiley taught 10th grade English at an inner-city Los Angeles high school through the Teach For America program. He subsequently worked as an attorney, including prosecuting a civil case against Huawei for intellectual property theft.

Political Career

Kiley was elected to the California State Assembly in 2016, representing a Sacramento-area district, and served three terms through 2022. He ran in the 2021 California gubernatorial recall election against Governor Gavin Newsom. He was elected to Congress in 2022, representing California's 3rd District.

In Congress, Kiley chairs the House Education Subcommittee on Early Childhood, Elementary, and Secondary Education. He authored the PRESS Act, a federal shield law protecting journalists' confidential sources, which passed the House with bipartisan support. He was involved in the questioning that contributed to the firing of DHS Secretary Kristi Noem.

Party Change — March 2026

On March 9, 2026, Kiley announced he was changing his voter registration from Republican to Independent. He stated he would continue to caucus with House Republicans. He is running in the newly redrawn CA-6 district in 2026 under California's Proposition 50 redistricting maps.
