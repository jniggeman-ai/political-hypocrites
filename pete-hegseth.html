Documented ControversiesSource: The New Yorker · CNN · NYT · NBC · AP · Reuters · The Atlantic
Signal Chat — Leaked Classified Strike PlansConfirmed

In March 2025, The Atlantic's editor-in-chief Jeffrey Goldberg reported he was accidentally added to a Signal group chat in which Hegseth shared detailed operational plans for U.S. strikes against the Houthis in Yemen — including targets, weapons, and timing — before the strikes occurred. The information was widely described by national security experts as likely classified. In April 2025, it was further reported that Hegseth had shared similar strike details on a second Signal chat on his personal phone, which included his wife, lawyer, and brother.

Source: The Atlantic · AP · Reuters · NBC News

Sexual Assault Allegation — Monterey, California (2017)Settled

A woman filed a police report in Monterey, California alleging that Hegseth sexually assaulted her in his hotel room on October 8, 2017, at a GOP conference. Hegseth was never criminally charged. He acknowledged paying an undisclosed settlement to the woman. Hegseth strongly denies all allegations, stating "I'm not a perfect person, but redemption is real."

Source: AP · Reuters · CNN · Monterey city attorney records

Financial Mismanagement at Veterans NonprofitsInvestigated

The New Yorker reported that Hegseth was pushed out as executive director of both Vets for Freedom and Concerned Veterans for America amid internal allegations of financial mismanagement and personal misconduct, including excessive drinking. NBC News separately reported that his drinking habits concerned his colleagues at Fox News. The New Yorker's reporting was a significant threat to his confirmation.

Source: The New Yorker · NBC News · OpenSecrets

Mother's Email — Accusation of Mistreating WomenDocumented

The New York Times reported that Hegseth's own mother, Penelope Hegseth, wrote him an email on April 30, 2018, accusing him of mistreating his former wife Samantha Deering and other women, calling him a "sinner" and "predator." She later told the Times she regretted sending the email and said her son had changed. Hegseth has been married three times.

Source: The New York Times

"Insider Threat" Flag — Jerusalem Cross TattooDocumented

In 2021, Hegseth was removed from inauguration security duty for President Biden after a fellow National Guard member flagged him as a possible "insider threat" due to his tattoos — including a Jerusalem cross and a "Deus Vult" (God Wills It) symbol, the latter of which has been used by white nationalist groups. According to Reuters and AP, at least two of his tattoos depict symbols associated with Christian nationalism. Hegseth resigned from the military following this incident.

Source: Reuters · AP · Snopes fact-check

Renamed Pentagon to "Department of War"Policy Action

On September 5, 2025, Hegseth joined Trump in the Oval Office for the signing of an executive order rebranding the Pentagon as the "Department of War." Hegseth said the name change was "not just about renaming, it's about restoring."

Source: CNN · AP · official EO records

Alleged War Crimes — Caribbean Drug Trafficker Strikes (2025)Under Review

Hegseth has drawn criticism for alleged war crimes related to U.S. military strikes on alleged drug traffickers in the Caribbean Sea, including the use of unmarked military planes for boat strikes and questions about rules of engagement and civilian casualties.

Source: Wikipedia · DOD records · AP

Promoted Christianity Inside the MilitaryPolicy Action

Hegseth has promoted Christianity inside the military, including Christian nationalist rhetoric that has drawn scrutiny — particularly after the U.S. entered direct military conflict with Iran in early 2026. $25–45M Military Parade (Trump Birthday)Policy Decision

Hegseth supported and defended the cost of a military parade held on June 14, 2025 — estimated at $25–45 million — to celebrate the 250th anniversary of the U.S. Army. The event grew substantially at Trump's request, whose birthday is June 14. Costs included repairs to street damage from heavy military machinery.

Source: Britannica · AP · DOD

Hegseth has no direct financial ties to Donald Trump beyond being a consistent Republican donor. He made a $1,000 contribution to House candidate Ian Smith in New Jersey. His financial controversies stem from his nonprofit management record, not political donations.

Nonprofit Financial Controversies: The New Yorker reported allegations of financial mismanagement at both Vets for Freedom and Concerned Veterans for America during his tenure. The DOD's budget — nearly $1 trillion annually — is now under his oversight despite no prior experience managing a large organization.

Koch Network Connection: Concerned Veterans for America, which Hegseth led, was heavily funded by the Koch Brothers network — a major source of libertarian-leaning Republican funding that has historically advocated for reducing U.S. military intervention abroad.
